## 16.1 European derivatives and replicating strategies
- **European derivative:** identified by its payoff at maturity $T$, $X(T)$, with $\mathbb{E}^Q[X(T)^2]<\infty$.
- **Replicating strategy** for $X(T)$: $\vartheta=(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1)$ such that (1) $\vartheta$ is self-financing; (2) $V_\vartheta(T)=X(T)$.

## 16.2 The pricing problem
BS market ($B$, $S$) is NA and complete. Introduce a European derivative with payoff $X(T)$, price process $S_X(t)$, $0\le t\le T$. **NA-pricing problem:** determine $S_X$ so that the extended market ($B$, $S$, derivative) remains arbitrage-free.

> ### ★ EXAM — Proposition 177 [BOR26] (No-arbitrage valuation in the BS market) — statement only
> Let $X(T)$ be the payoff of a European derivative and $S_X=\{S_X(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}$ its price process. The following are equivalent:
> 1. **Arbitrage-free market:** the extended market with $B$, $S$, and the derivative traded at $S_X$ is arbitrage-free.
> 2. **Replication:** if $\vartheta$ is self-financing with $V_\vartheta(T)=X(T)$, then $S_X(t)=V_\vartheta(t)$ for all $t\in[0,T]$.
> 3. **Risk-neutral valuation:**
> $$S_X(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q\left[e^{-r(T-t)}X(T)\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_t\right],\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],$$
> equivalently $\tilde S_X(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q[\tilde X(T)\mid\mathcal{F}_t]$ ⇒ $\tilde S_X$ is a $Q$-**martingale**.

- Item 3 gives a direct, efficient way to **compute** the price. Item 2: the replicating strategy is recovered from the BS PDE (for payoffs depending only on $S(T)$).

## 16.3 Price as a function of $(t,S(t))$ — Proposition 178 [BOR26]
If $X(T)=f(S(T))$ with $f:\mathbb{R}_+\to\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{E}^Q[X(T)^2]<\infty$, then
$$S_X(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q[e^{-r(T-t)}X(T)\mid\mathcal{F}_t]=F(t,S(t)),$$
$$F(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^Q\left[e^{-r(T-t)}f\left(x\,e^{(r-\frac12\sigma^2)(T-t)+\sigma(W^Q(T)-W^Q(t))}\right)\right]$$
($W^Q(T)-W^Q(t)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,T-t)$ independent of $\mathcal{F}_t$ ⇒ Markov: the price depends only on today's $S(t)$.)
Practical form: $F(t,x)=e^{-r\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\big(xe^{(r-\frac12\sigma^2)\tau+\sigma\sqrt\tau z}\big)\phi(z)\,dz$ with $\tau=T-t$.

## 16.4 Black–Scholes call formula

> ### ★ EXAM — Proposition 179 [BOR26] (European call option) — statement only
> A European call with maturity $T$ and strike $K$, payoff $(S(T)-K)^+$, has time-$t$ NA price
> $$c(t)=S(t)\,N(d_1)-Ke^{-r(T-t)}N(d_2),$$
> where $N(y)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^y\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2}dz$ is the standard normal cdf and
> $$d_1=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}\left[\ln\frac{S(t)}{K}+\left(r+\tfrac12\sigma^2\right)(T-t)\right],\qquad d_2=d_1-\sigma\sqrt{T-t}.$$

**Proof at $t=0$ (from the slides — useful technique for exotic payoffs):**
1. $c(0)=\mathbb{E}^Q[e^{-rT}\max(S(T)-K,0)]$, with $S(T)=S_0e^{(r-\frac12\sigma^2)T+\sigma\sqrt TZ}$, $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ under $Q$.
2. $c(0)=e^{-rT}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\max\big(S_0e^{(r-\frac12\sigma^2)T+\sigma\sqrt Tz}-K,0\big)\phi(z)\,dz$, $\phi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2}$.
3. Payoff positive iff $z>z^*=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt T}\left[-\ln\frac{S_0}{K}-(r-\frac12\sigma^2)T\right]=-d_2$.
4. $c(0)=e^{-rT}(I_1-I_2)$ with $I_2=K\int_{z^*}^\infty\phi(z)dz=KN(-z^*)=KN(d_2)$ and
   $I_1=S_0e^{(r-\frac12\sigma^2)T}\int_{z^*}^\infty e^{\sigma\sqrt Tz}\phi(z)dz=S_0e^{rT}\int_{z^*}^\infty\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac12(z-\sigma\sqrt T)^2}dz$ (completing the square).
5. Change of variable $y=z-\sigma\sqrt T$: $I_1=S_0e^{rT}N(-y^*)$, $y^*=z^*-\sigma\sqrt T=-(d_2+\sigma\sqrt T)=-d_1$ ⇒ $I_1=S_0e^{rT}N(d_1)$.
6. $c(0)=S_0N(d_1)-Ke^{-rT}N(d_2)$. For time $t$: replace $T$ by the residual life $T-t$ and $S_0$ by $S(t)$. ∎

**Interpretation:** $N(d_2)=Q[S(T)>K]$ = RN probability of exercise; $N(d_1)$ = delta of the call; $S_0N(d_1)=e^{-rT}\mathbb{E}^Q[S(T)\mathbf 1_{\{S(T)>K\}}]$ (value of receiving the stock if exercised); $Ke^{-rT}N(d_2)$ = value of paying $K$ if exercised.

## 16.5 Put formula

> ### ★ EXAM — Proposition 181 [BOR26] (European put option) — statement only
> A European put with maturity $T$ and strike $K$, payoff $(K-S(T))^+$, has time-$t$ NA price
> $$p(t)=Ke^{-r(T-t)}N(-d_2)-S(t)\,N(-d_1),$$
> with $N$, $d_1$, $d_2$ as for the call.

**Put–call parity (continuous time):** $(S(T)-K)^+-(K-S(T))^+=S(T)-K$ ⇒ long call − long put replicates long stock − borrow $Ke^{-r(T-t)}$:
$$c(t)-p(t)=S(t)-Ke^{-r(T-t)}.$$
Using the call formula and $1-N(x)=N(-x)$: $p(t)=S(t)(N(d_1)-1)-Ke^{-r(T-t)}(N(d_2)-1)=Ke^{-r(T-t)}N(-d_2)-S(t)N(-d_1)$.
$Q[S(T)<K]=N(-d_2)$ = RN probability that the put ends in the money.

## 16.6 Black–Scholes PDE

> ### ★ EXAM — Proposition 182 [BOR26] (Black–Scholes PDE) — statement **and historical derivation (Sec. 16.2.3)**
> Consider a European derivative whose payoff at $T$ is a function of the underlying, $X(T)=f(S(T))$, with $f:\mathbb{R}_+\to\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{E}^Q[X(T)^2]<\infty$. Let $F$ be the NA pricing function, $S_X(t)=F(t,S(t))$, and assume $F$ is $C^1$ in $t$ and $C^2$ in $S$. Then $F$ satisfies, for all $0<t<T$ and $S>0$:
> $$\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(t,S)+rS\frac{\partial F}{\partial S}(t,S)+\frac12\sigma^2S^2\frac{\partial^2F}{\partial S^2}(t,S)=rF(t,S),$$
> with **terminal condition** $F(T,S)=f(S)$ for all $S>0$.
> (For a European call: $F(T,S)=(S-K)^+$; for a put: $F(T,S)=(K-S)^+$.)

**Two derivations:** (a) *risk-neutral* (Sec. 16.2.2, not required): discounted prices are $Q$-martingales ⇒ the $dt$-term of $d(e^{-rt}F)$ under $Q$ must vanish. (b) ***Classical/historical (Sec. 16.2.3, REQUIRED):*** build a self-financing, **locally risk-free** portfolio (short derivative + stock); NA ⇒ it earns $r$.

### Historical (hedging) derivation — reproduce at the exam
1. **Setup.** Assume the derivative price is $F(t,S(t))$. Under the historical measure $P$:
$$dS(t)=\mu S(t)\,dt+\sigma S(t)\,dW^P(t).$$
   Build the **hedging portfolio**: **short one derivative** and hold $h(t)$ units of the stock:
$$\pi(t)=h(t)S(t)-F(t,S(t)).$$
2. **Itô's formula for the derivative** (all derivatives at $(t,S(t))$):
$$dF(t,S(t))=\left(F_t+\mu S(t)F_S+\tfrac12\sigma^2S(t)^2F_{SS}\right)dt+\sigma S(t)F_S\,dW^P(t).$$
   (The $dt$ term = local drift of the derivative; the $dW^P$ term = its local risk exposure.)
3. **Variation of the (self-financing) portfolio:** $d\pi(t)=h(t)\,dS(t)-dF(t,S(t))$. Substituting:
$$d\pi(t)=\left[-F_t+(h-F_S)\mu S(t)-\tfrac12\sigma^2S(t)^2F_{SS}\right]dt+(h-F_S)\sigma S(t)\,dW^P(t).$$
4. **Eliminate local risk:** to make $\pi$ locally risk-free the $dW^P$ term must vanish: $(h-F_S)\sigma S(t)=0$ ⇒
$$h(t)=F_S(t,S(t))\qquad(\textbf{delta hedge}).$$
   This removes the Brownian risk over $[t,t+dt]$.
5. **Locally risk-free portfolio:** with $h=F_S$:
$$d\pi(t)=\left(-F_t-\tfrac12\sigma^2S(t)^2F_{SS}\right)dt,\qquad \pi(t)=F_S(t,S(t))S(t)-F(t,S(t)).$$
6. **No-arbitrage condition:** a locally risk-free portfolio must earn the risk-free rate (otherwise borrow/lend at $r$ against it ⇒ arbitrage):
$$d\pi(t)=r\pi(t)\,dt=r\big(F_S(t,S(t))S(t)-F(t,S(t))\big)dt.$$
7. **Obtain the PDE:** equating the two expressions for $d\pi$:
$$-F_t-\tfrac12\sigma^2S(t)^2F_{SS}=r(F_SS(t)-F)\ \Longrightarrow\ F_t+rS(t)F_S+\tfrac12\sigma^2S(t)^2F_{SS}=rF.$$
   This must hold for all $t\in[0,T]$ and all values $S(t)$; since $S(t)$ can take any positive value, it is equivalent to require it for all $S>0$:
$$F_t(t,S)+rSF_S(t,S)+\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2F_{SS}(t,S)=rF(t,S).$$
   At maturity the derivative is worth its payoff ⇒ $F(T,S)=f(S)$. ∎

**Why $\mu$ disappears:** the PDE is derived from the historical dynamics, yet it doesn't depend on $\mu$. The delta hedge removes the risky term and with it the stock's risk premium; once the portfolio is locally riskless, NA forces its return to be exactly $r$. ⇒ **Two investors with different views on $\mu$ agree on the option price.**

**Hedge ratio (Delta):** $\Delta(t)=\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial S}(t,S(t))$ = number of shares needed to hedge one short derivative = sensitivity of the derivative price to the stock. Rebalancing it continuously replicates the derivative. Call: $\Delta=N(d_1)\in(0,1)$; put: $\Delta=N(d_1)-1=-N(-d_1)\in(-1,0)$.

## 16.7 Replicating strategy

> ### ★ EXAM — Proposition 184 [BOR26] (Replicating strategy in $B$ and $S$) — statement only
> The self-financing replicating strategy at $t$ is
> $$\vartheta_1(t)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial S}(t,S(t)),\qquad\vartheta_0(t)=\big(F(t,S(t))-\vartheta_1(t)S(t)\big)e^{-rt}.$$
> $\vartheta_1$ = stock holding (delta); $\vartheta_0$ = units of the risk-free asset ($\vartheta_0(t)B(t) = F-\vartheta_1S$ = **amount** invested in the bond).

- Replicating strategy is **self-financing**, **not** locally riskless (it replicates a risky derivative), and does **not** always have a long bond position (call: $\vartheta_0<0$, borrowing; put: $\vartheta_0>0$, lending; call: $\vartheta_1=N(d_1)>0$; put: $\vartheta_1=-N(-d_1)<0$).
- For the call: $\vartheta_0(t)B(t) = -Ke^{-r(T-t)}N(d_2)$; for the put: $\vartheta_0(t)B(t)=Ke^{-r(T-t)}N(-d_2)$.

## 16.8 PDE and risk-neutral valuation (Feynman–Kac)
Under regularity conditions, the solution of the BS PDE with terminal condition $F(T,S)=f(S)$ is
$$F(t,x)=e^{-r(T-t)}\mathbb{E}^Q[f(S(T))\mid S(t)=x].$$
**Check if a function solves the PDE** (frequent exercise): compute $F_t$, $F_S$, $F_{SS}$, substitute, and check the terminal condition. Examples of solutions: $F=S$ (the stock), $F=e^{-r(T-t)}$ (zero-coupon bond), $F = S - Ke^{-r(T-t)}$ (forward position), $F=S^n e^{a(T-t)}$ with $a=(n-1)(r+\frac12n\sigma^2)$ (power claim $S(T)^n$).

## 16.9 Market price of risk — Conclusion 186 [BOR26]
If two risky assets depend on the same Brownian motion, under NA
$$\frac{\mu_1-r}{\sigma_1}=\frac{\mu_2-r}{\sigma_2}=\nu.$$
$\nu$ = **market price of risk**: identical across all assets exposed to the same source of uncertainty (including derivatives: a derivative $F$ has $\mu_F = \frac{F_t+\mu SF_S+\frac12\sigma^2S^2F_{SS}}{F}$, $\sigma_F = \frac{\sigma SF_S}{F}$, and $\frac{\mu_F - r}{\sigma_F} = \frac{\mu-r}{\sigma}$). Proof idea: combine the two assets to cancel $dW$ ⇒ locally riskless ⇒ must earn $r$.

## 16.10 Toolbox for exotic payoffs (frequent exam exercises)
With $\tau=T-t$, $d_{1,2}$ computed with strike $K$, under $Q$:

| Payoff at $T$ | Price at $t$ |
|---|---|
| $S(T)$ | $S(t)$ |
| $1$ (ZCB) | $e^{-r\tau}$ |
| $S(T)-K$ (forward) | $S(t)-Ke^{-r\tau}$ |
| $(S(T)-K)^+$ | $S(t)N(d_1)-Ke^{-r\tau}N(d_2)$ |
| $(K-S(T))^+$ | $Ke^{-r\tau}N(-d_2)-S(t)N(-d_1)$ |
| $\mathbf 1_{\{S(T)>K\}}$ (cash-or-nothing digital call) | $e^{-r\tau}N(d_2)$ |
| $\mathbf 1_{\{S(T)<K\}}$ (digital put) | $e^{-r\tau}N(-d_2)$ |
| $S(T)\mathbf 1_{\{S(T)>K\}}$ (asset-or-nothing call) | $S(t)N(d_1)$ |
| $S(T)\mathbf 1_{\{S(T)<K\}}$ (asset-or-nothing put) | $S(t)N(-d_1)$ |
| $\mathbf 1_{\{K_1<S(T)<K_2\}}$ | $e^{-r\tau}[N(d_2(K_1))-N(d_2(K_2))]$ |
| $S(T)^n$ | $S(t)^ne^{(n-1)(r+\frac12n\sigma^2)\tau}$ |
| $\ln S(T)$ | $e^{-r\tau}[\ln S(t)+(r-\frac12\sigma^2)\tau]$ |

- **Decomposition principle (linearity of pricing):** write an exotic payoff as a combination of the above (e.g. gap put $(K_2-S)\mathbf 1_{\{S<K_1\}} = (K_1-S)^+ + (K_2-K_1)\mathbf 1_{\{S<K_1\}}$; bull spread $(S-K_1)^+-(S-K_2)^+$; straddle $(S-K)^++(K-S)^+$).
- **Probabilities:** $Q[S(T)>K]=N(d_2)$; $P[S(T)>K]=N(\hat d_2)$ where $\hat d_2$ uses $\mu$ instead of $r$.
- **Historical expectations of payoffs:** replace $r$ by $\mu$ in the "undiscounted" formulas: $\mathbb{E}^P[(S(T)-K)^+]=S_0e^{\mu T}N(\hat d_1)-KN(\hat d_2)$ with $\hat d_{1,2}=\frac{\ln(S_0/K)+(\mu\pm\frac12\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T}$.
- **Greeks (useful extra):** call $\Delta=N(d_1)$, $\Gamma=\frac{\phi(d_1)}{S\sigma\sqrt\tau}$, $\mathcal{V}=S\phi(d_1)\sqrt\tau$, $\Theta = -\frac{S\phi(d_1)\sigma}{2\sqrt\tau}-rKe^{-r\tau}N(d_2)$, $\rho = K\tau e^{-r\tau}N(d_2)$. In terms of Greeks the PDE reads $\Theta + rS\Delta + \frac12\sigma^2S^2\Gamma = rF$.
- **Limits:** as $\sigma\to0$ or $\tau\to0$, $c\to(S-Ke^{-r\tau})^+$; as $\sigma\to\infty$, $c\to S$. Bounds: $(S-Ke^{-r\tau})^+\le c\le S$.
- **Implied volatility:** the $\sigma$ that equates the BS price to the market price (call price is increasing in $\sigma$ ⇒ unique solution; found numerically).

8. *(4) Inverted strikes $K_1=20>K_2=10$:* $(10-S)^+\mathbf 1_{\{S<20\}}=(10-S)^+$ (if $S<10$ then automatically $S<20$) ⇒ it is a plain put with strike 10: price $=p(0)=8.187N(-0.15)-10N(-0.65)$.
