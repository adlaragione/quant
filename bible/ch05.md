# Chapter 5 — The One-Period Binomial Model

> No items of this chapter are on the official theory list, but **the binomial model is the backbone of the numerical exercises** (one-period and multi-period trees).

## 5.1 Why the binomial model matters
Simplest one-period market: **2 states**, **1 risky asset**, **1 risk-free asset**. A laboratory for NA, completeness, risk-neutral valuation, replication and hedging; building block of the **multi-period binomial model** and, in the limit, of **Black–Scholes**.

## 5.2 Assumptions
- $\Omega = \{\omega_1,\omega_2\}$.
- Risk-free asset: $B(0)=1$, $B(1)=1+r$, $r>-1$.
- Risky asset: $S(0) = S>0$, $S(1)(\omega_1)=uS$, $S(1)(\omega_2)=dS$, with $u>d$ (and $d>0$ so prices stay positive).
- $u$ = gross return in the up state ($u-1$ net return); $d$ = gross return in the down state. $u\ne d$ ensures the asset is really risky.

Event tree:
```
 B:  1 ──> 1+r          S:  S ──> uS   (ω1)
       └─> 1+r                └─> dS   (ω2)
```

## 5.3 Payoff matrix and completeness
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1+r & uS\\ 1+r & dS\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det A = (1+r)S(d-u) \neq 0.$$
Since $1+r>0$, $S>0$, $d<u$ ⇒ $\operatorname{rank}A=2=K$ ⇒ **the one-period binomial market is always complete.**

**LOP:** columns linearly independent ⇒ no non-zero portfolio with zero payoff ⇒ LOP always holds. (Completeness alone does not imply NA.)

## 5.4 No-arbitrage condition
$Q=(q_1,q_2)$ solves
$$q_1+q_2=1,\qquad \frac{q_1 uS + q_2 dS}{1+r} = S,$$
$$\boxed{q_1 = q_u = \frac{(1+r)-d}{u-d}, \qquad q_2 = q_d = \frac{u-(1+r)}{u-d}.}$$
**NA in the binomial model ⟺ $d < 1+r < u$** (⟺ $q_1,q_2>0$).
- If $1+r\ge u$: the stock is dominated by the bond (short stock, invest in bond: arbitrage).
- If $1+r \le d$: the bond is dominated by the stock (borrow, buy stock: arbitrage).

**2nd FTAP in the binomial model:** complete + $d<1+r<u$ ⟺ unique RN probability.

## 5.5 European call
Payoff: $X(\omega_1) = (uS-E)^+$, $X(\omega_2)=(dS-E)^+$. Attainable (complete market), unique replicating strategy $\vartheta^c = (\vartheta^c_0,\vartheta^c_1)$ solving
$$\begin{pmatrix}1+r & uS\\1+r & dS\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\vartheta^c_0\\ \vartheta^c_1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(uS-E)^+\\(dS-E)^+\end{pmatrix}$$
$$\vartheta^c_1 = \frac{(uS-E)^+-(dS-E)^+}{S(u-d)} \;(\text{the "delta"} = \Delta c/\Delta S),\qquad \vartheta^c_0 = \frac{u(dS-E)^+ - d(uS-E)^+}{(1+r)(u-d)}.$$
- Under the assumptions: $\vartheta^c_1\ge0$, $\vartheta^c_0\le0$: the call writer hedges by **buying stock** and **borrowing**.
- Cases: always OTM ($E\ge uS$): $\vartheta^c = 0$; always ITM ($E\le dS$): $\vartheta^c_1 = 1$, $\vartheta^c_0 = -E/(1+r)$; intermediate ($dS<E<uS$): $0<\vartheta^c_1<1$.

**Call price** = cost of replication:
$$c = \vartheta^c_0 + \vartheta^c_1 S = \frac{1}{1+r}\left[\frac{(1+r)-d}{u-d}(uS-E)^+ + \frac{u-(1+r)}{u-d}(dS-E)^+\right] = \frac{1}{1+r}\left[q_1(uS-E)^+ + q_2(dS-E)^+\right].$$
Risk-neutral pricing is simpler but gives only the price, not the hedge.

## 5.6 European put
Payoff $X(\omega_1)=(E-uS)^+$, $X(\omega_2)=(E-dS)^+$.
$$\vartheta^p_1 = \frac{(E-uS)^+-(E-dS)^+}{S(u-d)}\le0,\qquad \vartheta^p_0 = \frac{u(E-dS)^+-d(E-uS)^+}{(1+r)(u-d)}\ge0.$$
- Put writer hedges by **investing in the bond** and **short-selling stock**.
- Always exercised ($E\ge uS$): $\vartheta^p_0 = E/(1+r)$, $\vartheta^p_1=-1$. Intermediate: $0<\vartheta^p_0<E/(1+r)$, $-1<\vartheta^p_1<0$.
$$p = \vartheta^p_0+\vartheta^p_1S = \frac{1}{1+r}\left[q_1(E-uS)^+ + q_2(E-dS)^+\right].$$

## 5.7 Put–call parity
$$\boxed{p = c - S + \frac{E}{1+r}}$$
Since $(S(1)-E)^+ - (E-S(1))^+ = S(1)-E$ in every state: a put is replicated by **buying one call, short-selling one share, investing $E/(1+r)$ in the bond**. Equality of prices follows from the LOP.

## 5.8 General recipe (any payoff $X$)
$$\Delta = \vartheta_1 = \frac{X_u - X_d}{uS - dS},\qquad \vartheta_0 = \frac{uX_d - dX_u}{(1+r)(u-d)},\qquad S_X(0) = \frac{q_uX_u+q_dX_d}{1+r}.$$

## 5.9 Takeaways
- Always complete (under $u>d$). NA ⟺ $d<1+r<u$.
- Unique $Q$: $q_u = \frac{1+r-d}{u-d}$, $q_d = \frac{u-1-r}{u-d}$.
- Calls/puts priced by replication or by discounted $Q$-expectation; linked by put–call parity.
