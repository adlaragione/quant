## 7.1 Setting
- Finite horizon of dates $\mathcal{T} = \{0,1,\dots,T\}$.
- Finite set of **terminal scenarios** $\Omega = \{\omega_1,\dots,\omega_K\}$ with a strictly positive probability $P$.
- Key issue: **how information is progressively revealed** over time. Tools: information structures, measurable r.v.'s, conditional expectations, adapted processes, martingales.

## 7.2 Partitions — Definition 70 [BOR26]
A **partition** of $\Omega$ is a collection $\mathcal{A}=\{A_1,\dots,A_\ell\}$ of subsets with $A_i\cap A_j=\emptyset$ ($i\ne j$) and $\bigcup_i A_i = \Omega$. Each **cell** groups scenarios that are **indistinguishable** at a given date.

## 7.3 Finer / coarser — Definition 72 [BOR26]
$\mathcal{A}'$ is **finer** than $\mathcal{A}$ if every cell of $\mathcal{A}'$ is contained in some cell of $\mathcal{A}$. Finer = more information = smaller cells. Two partitions may be non-comparable.

## 7.4 Information structure — Definition 74 [BOR26]
An **information structure** on $\mathcal{T}$ and $\Omega$ is a family of partitions $\mathcal{P} = \{\mathcal{P}_t\}_{t=0}^T$ such that:
1. $\mathcal{P}_0 = \{\Omega\}$ (at time 0 only the list of possible scenarios is known);
2. $\mathcal{P}_{t+1}$ is finer than $\mathcal{P}_t$ for all $t$ (**perfect memory**: information is never forgotten);
3. $\mathcal{P}_T = \{\{\omega_1\},\dots,\{\omega_K\}\}$ (full revelation at $T$).

**Event tree:** each node is a cell $f_h^t$ of $\mathcal{P}_t$ (notation: $f^t_h$ = $h$-th cell at date $t$), each branch a refinement, terminal nodes are singletons. Example from slides ($T=3$, $K=7$):
```
t=0        t=1                 t=2             t=3
           {ω1,ω2,ω3} ──── {ω1,ω2} ──── {ω1},{ω2}
{Ω} ──┤                 └── {ω3}   ──── {ω3}
           {ω4,...,ω7} ─── {ω4,ω5} ──── {ω4},{ω5}
                        └── {ω6,ω7} ─── {ω6},{ω7}
```
(The number of successors of a node may differ across nodes.)

## 7.5 Information generated by prices
Information is often generated by **observed price histories**. Terminal scenarios must describe **entire price paths**, not just final prices: two paths ending at the same price are different scenarios if their histories differ (e.g. up-down vs down-up in a binomial tree are two different $\omega$'s even if $S(2)$ is the same). The partition at $t$ groups scenarios that are still indistinguishable given prices observed up to $t$.

## 7.6 Measurable random variables

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 77 [BOR26] ($\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable random variable)
> A random variable $Y:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ is **measurable with respect to $\mathcal{P}_t$** if it is **constant on every cell** of the partition $\mathcal{P}_t$: if $\omega',\omega''$ belong to the same cell $f_h^t$, then $Y(\omega') = Y(\omega'')$.

Hence $Y$ can be seen as a map $Y:\mathcal{P}_t\to\mathbb{R}$ (a value per node). Economically: **the value of $Y$ is already known at time $t$**.
- Every r.v. is $\mathcal{P}_T$-measurable; only constants are $\mathcal{P}_0$-measurable.

## 7.7 Conditional expectation

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 79 [BOR26] (Conditional expected value)
> Let $X$ be a random variable and $f_h^t$ a cell of $\mathcal{P}_t$. Then
> $$\mathbb{E}^P[X\mid\mathcal{P}_t](f_h^t) = \sum_{\omega\in f_h^t} X(\omega)\,\frac{P[\omega]}{P[f_h^t]}.$$

- It is the **best prediction** of $X$ given the information available at $t$; it is itself a **$\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable** r.v. (one value per node).
- $P[\omega]/P[f^t_h] = P[\omega\mid f^t_h]$ is the conditional probability of $\omega$ given the cell.
- Short notation: $\mathbb{E}^P_t[X] := \mathbb{E}^P[X\mid\mathcal{P}_t]$.

**Remark 80:** if $X$ is $\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable then $\mathbb{E}^P[X\mid\mathcal{P}_t] = X$.
**Remark 81:** at time 0, $\mathbb{E}^P[X\mid\mathcal{P}_0] = \mathbb{E}^P[X]$ (ordinary expectation). Conditional expectation interpolates between no information ($t=0$) and full revelation ($t=T$, where $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{P}_T]=X$).

## 7.8 Properties of conditional expectation

> ### ★ EXAM — Proposition 82 [BOR26] (Main properties)
> 1. **Law of iterated expectations (tower property):** $\mathbb{E}^P\big[\mathbb{E}^P[X\mid\mathcal{P}_{t_2}]\mid\mathcal{P}_{t_1}\big] = \mathbb{E}^P[X\mid\mathcal{P}_{t_1}]$, for $t_1\le t_2$;
> 2. **Taking out what is known:** $\mathbb{E}^P[h(t)X(s)\mid\mathcal{P}_t] = h(t)\,\mathbb{E}^P[X(s)\mid\mathcal{P}_t]$ if $h(t)$ is $\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable;
> 3. **Linearity:** $\mathbb{E}^P[h(t)X(s)+k(t)Y(s)\mid\mathcal{P}_t] = h(t)\mathbb{E}^P_t[X(s)] + k(t)\mathbb{E}^P_t[Y(s)]$ with $h(t),k(t)$ $\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable.

Special case of 1 with $t_1=0$: $\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{P}_t]] = \mathbb{E}[X]$.

## 7.9 Independence

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 84 [BOR26] (Independence from the information $\mathcal{P}_t$)
> A random variable $X$ is **independent of $\mathcal{P}_t$ under $P$** if, for every value $x_m$ of $X$ and every cell $f_h^t\in\mathcal{P}_t$,
> $$P\big[\{\omega: X(\omega)=x_m\}\cap f_h^t\big] = P\big[\{\omega:X(\omega)=x_m\}\big]\;P[f_h^t].$$

Equivalently $P[X=x_m\mid f_h^t] = P[X=x_m]$: knowing which cell has realized gives no extra information on the distribution of $X$.

> ### ★ EXAM — Proposition 86 [BOR26] — statement only
> If $X$ is independent of $\mathcal{P}_t$ with respect to $P$, then for every cell $f_h^t\in\mathcal{P}_t$,
> $$\mathbb{E}^P[X\mid\mathcal{P}_t](f_h^t) = \mathbb{E}^P[X].$$

The best forecast based on time-$t$ information is just the unconditional mean: information at $t$ is irrelevant for predicting $X$.

## 7.10 Adapted processes — Definition 88 [BOR26]
A stochastic process $X=\{X(t)\}_{t=0}^T$ is **adapted** to $\mathcal{P}$ if, for every $t$, $X(t)$ is $\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable. Values are inferable from current information. Prices, holdings, wealth are adapted processes.

**Remark 90 (equivalent view):** an adapted process is a map $X:\mathcal{T}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ such that $X(t,\cdot)$ is constant on every cell of $\mathcal{P}_t$. For fixed $\omega$, $X(0)(\omega),X(1)(\omega),\dots,X(T)(\omega)$ is the **trajectory (sample path)**. A process = a family of paths leading to terminal scenarios.

## 7.11 Martingales

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 91 [BOR26] (Martingale)
> Let $M=\{M(t)\}_{t=0}^T$ be **adapted** to $\mathcal{P}$. $M$ is a **martingale under $P$** if, for all $t_1<t_2$,
> $$\mathbb{E}^P[M(t_2)\mid\mathcal{P}_{t_1}] = M(t_1).$$

The current value is the best predictor of every future value ("fair game", constant on average).
- **Proposition 92 (constant mean):** if $M$ is a martingale, $\mathbb{E}^P[M(t)] = M(0)$ for all $t$. (Proof: tower property with $t_1=0$.) *Converse is false.*
- **Proposition 93 (one-step characterization):** $M$ is a martingale ⟺ $\mathbb{E}^P[M(t+1)\mid\mathcal{P}_t] = M(t)$ for all $t=0,\dots,T-1$. (Proof of ⇐: by the tower property, $\mathbb{E}[M(t+2)\mid\mathcal{P}_t] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[M(t+2)\mid\mathcal{P}_{t+1}]\mid\mathcal{P}_t] = \mathbb{E}[M(t+1)\mid\mathcal{P}_t] = M(t)$, iterate.)
- Related: **supermartingale** $\mathbb{E}[M(t_2)\mid\mathcal{P}_{t_1}]\le M(t_1)$; **submartingale** $\ge$.

**Useful martingale construction (Doob martingale):** for any r.v. $X$, $M(t)=\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{P}_t]$ is a martingale (tower property). This is why NA price processes $\tilde S_X(t) = \mathbb{E}^Q[\tilde X\mid\mathcal{P}_t]$ are $Q$-martingales.

## 7.12 Why it matters in finance
Information structures = what investors know; adapted processes = feasible prices and trading; conditional expectations = forecasts consistent with information; **martingales are central because, under risk-neutral probabilities, discounted price processes are martingales.**

## 7.13 Exercise techniques
- **Conditional probabilities on a tree:** $P[\omega\mid f^t_h] = P[\omega]/P[f^t_h]$; $P[f^t_h]=\sum_{\omega\in f^t_h}P[\omega]$.
- **Global probability from local (one-step) probabilities:** multiply along the path: $P[\omega_k] = \prod_{t} P[\text{successor}\mid\text{node}]$.
- **Check martingale property:** at each node verify value = conditional expectation of next-step values.
- **Check independence:** compare $P[X=x\mid f^t_h]$ across cells.

### Theory questions

**[Jan 16, 2023 – General, Q3 (6 pts)]** *Consider a (non-constant) $\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable r.v. $X$. Then… (mark all)*
A. its $\mathcal{P}_t$-conditional expectation equals its unconditional expectation — B. its $\mathcal{P}_t$-conditional expectation equals $X$ itself — C. $X$ takes all its possible realizations on all $f^t_h\in\mathcal{P}_t$ — D. $X$ is constant on each $f^t_h\in\mathcal{P}_t$.
**Answer: B, D.** D is Definition 77; B is Remark 80. A would hold for $X$ independent of $\mathcal{P}_t$ (Prop. 86), not measurable (and non-constant). C is the opposite of measurability.

**[Feb 1, 2023, Q3 (4 pts)]** *$X:\Omega\to\{x_1,\dots,x_M\}$ independent of $\mathcal{P}_t$ under $Q$, with $Q[\{X=x_m\}]>0$ for all $m$. Then:* A. $\mathbb{E}^Q[X|\mathcal{P}_t]=\mathbb{E}^Q[X]$ — B. $\mathbb{E}^Q[X|\mathcal{P}_t]=X$ — C. $\{X=x_m\}\cap f^t_h\neq\emptyset$ for all $m$ and all $f^t_h\in\mathcal{P}_t$ — D. $Q[\{X=x_m\}\cap f^t_h]=1$ for all $m,h$.
**Answer: A, C.** A is Prop. 86. C: by independence $Q[\{X=x_m\}\cap f^t_h]=Q[X=x_m]\,Q[f^t_h]>0$ (both factors positive), so the intersection cannot be empty — every value of $X$ is still possible in every cell. B would require measurability; D is absurd (probabilities of disjoint events cannot all be 1).
