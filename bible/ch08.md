## 8.1 Setting
- Trading dates $\mathcal{T}=\{0,1,\dots,T\}$; terminal scenarios $\Omega=\{\omega_1,\dots,\omega_K\}$; information structure $\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{P}_t\}_{t=0}^T$.
- $N+1$ securities: one **locally risk-free** asset $B$ ($j=0$) and $N$ risky securities with **adapted** price processes $S_j=\{S_j(t)\}_{t=0}^T$. At date $t$ and node $f^t_h$ the price is $S_j(t)(f^t_h)$.

## 8.2 The locally risk-free asset
$B=\{B(t)\}$, $B(0)=1$, and the **locally risk-free rate**
$$r(t) = \frac{B(t+1)-B(t)}{B(t)},\qquad t=0,\dots,T-1,$$
is **adapted** (known at time $t$, at the current node). So the return over $[t,t+1]$ is known at $t$: **locally** riskless (one step ahead), but **not globally** riskless (future rates $r(t+1),\dots$ are random seen from 0).
- **Accumulation factor:** $B(t+1) = B(t)(1+r(t))$ ⇒ $B(t+1) = \prod_{\tau=0}^{t}(1+r(\tau))$ (product along the path). $B(t+1)$ is **$\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable** (predictable).
- $1/B(t+1)$ = discount factor from $t+1$ to $0$.
- **Globally risk-free special case:** $r(t)=r$ constant ⇒ $B(t)=(1+r)^t$.
- Exercise tip: $B(2)(\omega) = (1+r(0))(1+r(1)(f^1_h))$ where $f^1_h$ is the node at $t=1$ on the path of $\omega$.

## 8.3 Dynamic investment strategies
A **dynamic strategy** is $\vartheta=(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1,\dots,\vartheta_N)$ where each $\vartheta_j=\{\vartheta_j(t)\}_{t=0}^{T-1}$ is an **adapted** process.
- $\vartheta_j(t)$ = total position in asset $j$ **after rebalancing at $t$**, held over $[t,t+1[$.
- At $T$ the portfolio is liquidated (no new allocation chosen at $T$).

## 8.4 Value process

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 97 [BOR26] (Value process)
> Given a dynamic strategy $\vartheta$, its **value process** is $V_\vartheta=\{V_\vartheta(t)\}_{t=0}^T$ with
> $$V_\vartheta(t) = \vartheta_0(t)B(t) + \sum_{j=1}^N \vartheta_j(t)S_j(t),\qquad t=0,1,\dots,T-1,$$
> and terminal value
> $$V_\vartheta(T) = \vartheta_0(T-1)B(T) + \sum_{j=1}^N\vartheta_j(T-1)S_j(T).$$

- For $t<T$, $V_\vartheta(t)$ is the **cost of the portfolio chosen at $t$** (after rebalancing) — **not** the liquidation value of the portfolio chosen at $t-1$.
- At $T$, $V_\vartheta(T)$ is the **terminal liquidation value** of the portfolio chosen at $T-1$.

## 8.5 Cashflow process

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 98 [BOR26] (Cashflow process)
> Given a dynamic strategy $\vartheta$, its **cashflow process** is $C_\vartheta=\{C_\vartheta(t)\}_{t=0}^T$ with
> $$C_\vartheta(0) = -V_\vartheta(0),$$
> $$C_\vartheta(t) = \vartheta_0(t-1)B(t) + \sum_{j=1}^N\vartheta_j(t-1)S_j(t) - V_\vartheta(t),\qquad t=1,\dots,T-1,$$
> $$C_\vartheta(T) = V_\vartheta(T).$$

- $t=0$: minus the initial cost. Intermediate $t$: **liquidation value of the old portfolio minus cost of the new one**. $t=T$: final liquidation value.
- **Rebalancing form:** for $t=1,\dots,T-1$,
$$C_\vartheta(t) = [\vartheta_0(t-1)-\vartheta_0(t)]B(t) + \sum_{j=1}^N[\vartheta_j(t-1)-\vartheta_j(t)]S_j(t)$$
  (net flow from selling what you no longer hold / buying what you add).
- (If assets paid dividends, these would be added; the course's basic setting is dividend-free.)

## 8.6 Self-financing strategies

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 100 [BOR26] (Self-financing strategy)
> A dynamic strategy $\vartheta$ is **self-financing** if
> $$C_\vartheta(t) = 0,\qquad t=1,\dots,T-1.$$
> Equivalently:
> $$\vartheta_0(t-1)B(t)+\sum_{j=1}^N\vartheta_j(t-1)S_j(t) = V_\vartheta(t),\qquad t=1,\dots,T-1.$$

The liquidation value of the old portfolio exactly finances the new allocation: **no capital injected, no cash withdrawn** at intermediate dates. Only $C_\vartheta(0) = -V_\vartheta(0)$ and $C_\vartheta(T) = V_\vartheta(T)$ can be non-zero.

## 8.7 Buy-and-hold strategies — Definition 102 [BOR26]
$\vartheta$ is **buy-and-hold** if $\vartheta_j(t) = \vartheta_j(0)$ for $t=1,\dots,T-1$ and all $j=0,\dots,N$. Composition chosen at 0 and never changed (shorts allowed). With no dividends, **buy-and-hold strategies are self-financing** (rebalancing form gives $C_\vartheta(t)=0$).

## 8.8 Discounted gain process — Definition 104 [BOR26]
$$\tilde G_\vartheta(t) = \frac{V_\vartheta(t)}{B(t)} + \sum_{\tau=0}^t\frac{C_\vartheta(\tau)}{B(\tau)},\quad t=0,\dots,T-1;\qquad \tilde G_\vartheta(T) = \sum_{\tau=0}^T\frac{C_\vartheta(\tau)}{B(\tau)}.$$
- Aggregates all cashflows up to $t$ discounted to 0, plus (for $t<T$) the discounted current portfolio value.
- Note $\tilde G_\vartheta(0) = V_\vartheta(0) + C_\vartheta(0) = 0$.
- NA will be characterized by the existence of a measure under which discounted gain processes are martingales (Ch. 9).
- For a **self-financing** strategy: $\tilde G_\vartheta(t) = \tilde V_\vartheta(t) - V_\vartheta(0)$ for all $t$ (with $\tilde V = V/B$).

## 8.9 Law of one price

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 105 [BOR26] (Violation of the LOP, multi-period)
> A multi-period market **violates the law of one price** if there exist two dynamic strategies $\vartheta$ and $\vartheta'$ such that:
> - they generate the **same future cashflows**: $C_\vartheta(t)(f^t_h) = C_{\vartheta'}(t)(f^t_h)$ for all $t=1,\dots,T$ and all nodes $f^t_h$;
> - but they have **different initial values**: $V_\vartheta(0)\ne V_{\vartheta'}(0)$.

## 8.10 Arbitrage opportunities

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 106 [BOR26] (Arbitrage opportunity, multi-period)
> A dynamic strategy $\vartheta$ is an **arbitrage opportunity** if:
> 1. $V_\vartheta(0)\le 0$;
> 2. $C_\vartheta(t)(f^t_h)\ge0$ for all future dates $t=1,\dots,T$ and all nodes $f^t_h$;
> 3. and at least one of the following holds:
>    - **3I.** $C_\vartheta(\bar t)(f^{\bar t}_{\bar h}) > 0$ for some future node;
>    - **3II.** $V_\vartheta(0) < 0$.

Economic meaning: no positive initial investment, never negative future cashflows, strictly positive gain now or at some node. Investors would scale it up indefinitely ⇒ no equilibrium. Note arbitrage strategies need **not** be self-financing (cash may be withdrawn at intermediate dates).

## 8.11 LOP and arbitrage — Proposition 107 [BOR26]
Violations of the LOP imply the existence of arbitrage opportunities satisfying 1, 2 and 3II. Hence **LOP is necessary for NA**.

## 8.12 No-arbitrage — Definition 108 [BOR26]
A multi-period market satisfies **NA** if no arbitrage opportunities are available. Every NA market satisfies the LOP.

## 8.13 Key takeaways
Adapted prices and dynamic strategies; locally risk-free asset riskless only one step ahead; three processes per strategy (**value**, **cashflow**, **discounted gain**); self-financing strategies are central in derivative pricing; NA is the organizing principle.

## Past exam questions — Chapter 8

### Theory questions

**[Jan 2024 (2nd exam), Q2 (4 pts)]** *In the discrete multiperiod market with assets $B,S_1,\dots,S_N$, a self-financing strategy $\vartheta$ satisfies (mark all):* A. $\vartheta_0(t-1)B(t)+\sum_j\vartheta_j(t-1)S_j(t)=V_\vartheta(t)$, $t=1,\dots,T-1$ — B. $\{V_\vartheta(t)\}$ is a $Q$-martingale for any RN probability $Q$ — C. $V_\vartheta(t)=0$, $t=1,\dots,T-1$ — D. $C_\vartheta(t)=0$, $t=1,\dots,T-1$.
**Answer: A, D** (Definition 100 and its equivalent form). B is false: the **discounted** value $\tilde V_\vartheta=V_\vartheta/B$ is a $Q$-martingale, not $V_\vartheta$. C confuses value and cashflow.

**[Jan 29, 2025, Q2 (4 pts)]** *With discounted prices $\tilde S_j=S_j/B$ and discounted value $\tilde V_\vartheta$, any self-financing strategy satisfies (mark all):* A. $\vartheta_0(t-1)+\sum_j\vartheta_j(t-1)\tilde S_j(t)=\tilde V_\vartheta(t)$, $t=1,\dots,T-1$ — B. $\tilde V_\vartheta(t)=0$ — C. $\vartheta_0(t)+\sum_j\vartheta_j(t)\tilde S_j(t)=\tilde V_\vartheta(t)$, $t=0,\dots,T-1$ — D. $\tilde V_\vartheta(t)=\tilde V_\vartheta(0)$.
**Answer: A, C.** C is just the definition of value divided by $B(t)$ (true for **any** strategy); A is the self-financing condition (Def. 100) divided by $B(t)$. B and D are false (value is not constant: it changes with prices).
