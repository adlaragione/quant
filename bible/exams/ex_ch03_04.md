### [Jan 14, 2022 — General] Exercise 1 (45 points)

**Text.** Consider a one-period market with the riskless asset $B$ yielding a risk-free rate $r=2\%$, and a risky security $S$ whose prices at time $T=1$ are
$$S(1)(\omega_1)=15.3,\qquad S(1)(\omega_2)=5.1,\qquad S(1)(\omega_3)=10.2.$$

**1.** *(4 points)* Is the market complete? Justify your answer.

> **Solution.** No. The payoff matrix $A$ is $3\times2$, so $\operatorname{rank}A\le2<3=K$: there are fewer securities than scenarios, hence the market cannot be complete (Prop. 38).

**2.** *(6 points)* Suppose that $S$ trades at $t=0$ at the price $S(0)=7.5$. Let $Q(\omega_k)$, $k=1,2,3$, denote a risk-neutral probability. Determine $Q(\omega_2)$ and $Q(\omega_3)$ in terms of $Q(\omega_1)=q_1$. *(Mark the only appropriate alternative.)*
- **A.** $Q(\omega_2)=q_1$ and $Q(\omega_3)=1-2q_1$
- **B.** $Q(\omega_2)=\frac12-q_1$ and $Q(\omega_3)=\frac32 q_1$
- **C.** $Q(\omega_2)=q_1+\frac12$ and $Q(\omega_3)=\frac12-2q_1$
- **D.** $Q(\omega_2)=\frac12 q_1$ and $Q(\omega_3)=\frac12-2q_1$

> **Solution.** Impose $\frac{1}{1.02}[15.3q_1+5.1q_2+10.2(1-q_1-q_2)]=7.5$, i.e. $15q_1+5q_2+10(1-q_1-q_2)=7.5$ ⇒ $5q_1-5q_2=-2.5$ ⇒ $q_2=q_1+\frac12$, and $q_3=1-q_1-q_2=\frac12-2q_1$. **Answer C.**

**3.** *(3 points)* In order for $Q(\omega_1),Q(\omega_2),Q(\omega_3)$ to define a risk-neutral probability, $Q(\omega_1)=q_1$ must be strictly larger than …

**4.** *(3 points)* … and strictly smaller than …

> **Solution (3–4).** Impose positivity: $q_1>0$; $q_2=q_1+\frac12>0$ (automatic); $q_3=\frac12-2q_1>0\iff q_1<\frac14$. Hence $q_1$ must be **strictly larger than 0** and **strictly smaller than 0.25**.

**5.** *(4 points)* Is the market free of arbitrage opportunities? Justify your answer.

> **Solution.** Yes: risk-neutral probabilities exist (for every $q_1\in(0,0.25)$), hence the market is arbitrage-free by the 1st FTAP.

**6.** *(7 points)* Let $R(\omega_k)=\frac{S(1)(\omega_k)-S(0)}{S(0)}$ be the linear one-period return of $S$. A European derivative on $R$ with maturity $T=1$ is introduced, with payoff $X(1)(\omega_k)=1.02$ if $R(\omega_k)>0$, and $X(1)(\omega_k)=2.04$ otherwise. Is it possible to replicate this derivative with $S$ and $B$? Determine the set of no-arbitrage prices at $t=0$ for this derivative.

> **Solution.** $R(\omega_1)=1.04>0$, $R(\omega_2)=-0.32<0$, $R(\omega_3)=0.36>0$ ⇒ $X(1)=(1.02,\ 2.04,\ 1.02)$.
> $\det\begin{pmatrix}1.02&15.3&1.02\\1.02&5.1&2.04\\1.02&10.2&1.02\end{pmatrix}=5.31\neq0$ ⇒ $\operatorname{rank}[A|X]=3\neq2=\operatorname{rank}A$ ⇒ **NO, not replicable**.
> NA prices: $\frac{\mathbb{E}^Q[X]}{1.02}=q_1+2q_2+q_3=q_1+2q_1+1+\frac12-2q_1=q_1+\frac32$ with $q_1\in(0,0.25)$ ⇒ **NA price interval $(1.5,\ 1.75)$** (open).

**7.** *(4 points)* Formally describe the set of all strategies $\vartheta=(x,y)$ whose final value super-replicates $X(1)$, i.e. $V_\vartheta(1)(\omega_k)\ge X(1)(\omega_k)$ for all $k$.

> **Solution.** $1.02x+15.3y\ge1.02$, $1.02x+5.1y\ge2.04$, $1.02x+10.2y\ge1.02$, i.e.
> $$y\ge\tfrac{1-x}{15}\ (\omega_1),\qquad y\ge\tfrac{2-x}{5}\ (\omega_2),\qquad y\ge\tfrac{1-x}{10}\ (\omega_3).$$
> The set is $\{(x,y):y\ge\max\{\frac{1-x}{15},\frac{2-x}{5},\frac{1-x}{10}\}\}$. The binding envelope is the $\omega_2$ line for $x\le2.5$ and the $\omega_1$ line for $x\ge2.5$ (the $\omega_3$ constraint is never binding): $y\ge\frac{2-x}5$ if $x\le2.5$, $y\ge\frac{1-x}{15}$ if $x>2.5$.

**8.** *(2 points)* Among the strategies of Point 7, let $\vartheta^*=(x^*,y^*)$ be the one whose initial cashflow $C_\vartheta(0)$ is maximum. The riskless component $x^*$ is …

**9.** *(2 points)* The risky component $y^*$ is …

> **Solution (8–9).** Maximizing $C_\vartheta(0)=-(x+7.5y)$ means minimizing the cost $x+7.5y$. The iso-cost lines $y=\frac{c-x}{7.5}$ have slope $-\frac1{7.5}$, between the slopes of the binding constraints ($-\frac15$ and $-\frac1{15}$) ⇒ the optimum is at the kink: $\frac{2-x}{5}=\frac{1-x}{15}$ ⇒ **$x^*=2.5$, $y^*=-0.1$**. Cost $2.5-0.75=1.75$ = **upper bound** of the NA interval (LP duality, §4.6). Check: payoffs $(1.02,\,2.04,\,1.53)\ge X$ ✓.
> **Note on the official solution:** the official sheet states $x=3$, $y=-0.2$ with value $1.5$. That point violates the $\omega_1$ constraint ($3+15(-0.2)=0<1$), so it is not super-replicating; $1.5$ is the *lower* bound (best sub-replication: maximize $x+7.5y$ s.t. payoff $\le X$). Know both: **min super-replication cost $=1.75=\sup_Q$; max sub-replication value $=1.5=\inf_Q$.**

**10.** *(5 points)* Suppose the derivative of Point 6 trades at the initial price of $1.6$. The extended market (in which $B$, $S$ and the derivative are traded) is… *(Mark the only appropriate alternative.)*
- **A.** arbitrage-free AND complete
- **B.** not arbitrage-free AND complete
- **C.** arbitrage-free AND incomplete
- **D.** not arbitrage-free AND incomplete

> **Solution.** $1.6\in(1.5,1.75)$ ⇒ arbitrage-free; $X$ is non-redundant ⇒ adding it raises the rank to 3 ⇒ complete. **Answer A.**

**11.** *(5 points)* A new European derivative on $R$ is introduced in the extended market of Point 10, with payoff $Y(1)(\omega_k)=K$ if $R(\omega_k)>0$, $2K$ otherwise. Its initial NA price is $8$. In order to have an arbitrage-free market, $K$ has to be equal to …

> **Solution.** The extended market has a unique $Q$: $q_1+1.5=1.6$ ⇒ $q_1=0.1$, $q_2=0.6$, $q_3=0.3$. Then $8=\frac{0.1K+1.2K+0.3K}{1.02}=\frac{1.6K}{1.02}$ ⇒ **$K=5.1$** (equivalently $Y=\frac{K}{1.02}X$ ⇒ price $=\frac{K}{1.02}\cdot1.6$).

### [Jan 31, 2022 — General] Exercise 1 (45 points)

**Text.** Consider a one-period market with the riskless asset $B$ yielding a risk-free rate $r=5\%$, and a risky security $S$ whose prices at time $T=1$ are
$$S(1)(\omega_1)=12.6,\qquad S(1)(\omega_2)=10.5,\qquad S(1)(\omega_3)=8.4.$$

**1.** *(3 points)* Is the market complete? Justify your answer.

> **Solution.** No: 3 scenarios and only 2 securities ⇒ $\operatorname{rank}A\le2<3$.

**2.** *(4 points)* Suppose $S$ trades at $t=0$ at $S(0)=9.4$. Let $Q(\omega_k)$ denote a risk-neutral probability. Determine $Q(\omega_1)$ and $Q(\omega_2)$ in terms of $Q(\omega_3)=q_3$. *(Mark the only appropriate alternative.)*
- **A.** $Q(\omega_1)=\frac{3}{20}-q_3$ and $Q(\omega_2)=\frac{13}{10}-q_3$
- **B.** $Q(\omega_1)=\frac{3}{20}+2q_3$ and $Q(\omega_2)=\frac{13}{5}-q_3$
- **C.** $Q(\omega_1)=\frac{3}{10}-q_3$ and $Q(\omega_2)=\frac{13}{20}-q_3$
- **D.** $Q(\omega_1)=-\frac{3}{10}+q_3$ and $Q(\omega_2)=\frac{13}{10}-2q_3$

> **Solution.** $\frac{12.6q_1+10.5q_2+8.4q_3}{1.05}=9.4$ ⇔ $12q_1+10q_2+8q_3=9.4$; with $q_1=1-q_2-q_3$: $12-2q_2-4q_3=9.4$ ⇒ $q_2=1.3-2q_3$, $q_1=q_3-0.3$. **Answer D.**

**3.** *(2 points)* For $Q$ to be a RN probability, $q_3$ must be strictly larger than …

**4.** *(2 points)* … and strictly smaller than …

> **Solution (3–4).** $q_1=q_3-0.3>0\iff q_3>0.3$; $q_2=1.3-2q_3>0\iff q_3<0.65$ ⇒ $q_3\in(0.3,\ 0.65)$.

**5.** *(4 points)* Is the market free of arbitrage opportunities? Justify.

> **Solution.** Yes: RN probabilities exist ⇒ arbitrage-free (1st FTAP).

**6.** *(6 points)* A European call option on $S$ with maturity $T=1$ and strike $K_L=9.45$ is introduced. Is it possible to replicate it with $S$ and $B$? Determine the set of NA prices at $t=0$.

> **Solution.** Payoff $c_L(1)=(3.15,\ 1.05,\ 0)$. $\det\begin{pmatrix}1.05&12.6&3.15\\1.05&10.5&1.05\\1.05&8.4&0\end{pmatrix}=2.3153\neq0$ ⇒ **NO**. Price $=\frac{3.15(q_3-0.3)+1.05(1.3-2q_3)}{1.05}=q_3+0.4$, $q_3\in(0.3,0.65)$ ⇒ **NA interval $(0.7,\ 1.05)$**.

**7.** *(4 points)* Formally describe the set of all strategies $\vartheta=(x,y)$ whose final value super-replicates $X(1)=c_L(1)$.

> **Solution.** Dividing by $1.05$: $x+12y\ge3$, $x+10y\ge1$, $x+8y\ge0$, i.e. $y\ge\max\{\frac{3-x}{12},\frac{1-x}{10},-\frac{x}{8}\}$. The envelope is the $\omega_1$ line for $x\le-6$ and the $\omega_3$ line for $x>-6$: $y\ge\frac{3-x}{12}$ if $x\le-6$; $y\ge-\frac x8$ if $x>-6$.

**8.** *(2 points)* Among the strategies of Point 7, let $\vartheta^*=(x^*,y^*)$ be the one whose initial value $V_\vartheta(0)$ is minimum. The riskless component $x^*$ is …

**9.** *(2 points)* The risky component $y^*$ is …

> **Solution (8–9).** Iso-cost lines $x+9.4y=c$; the optimum is at the kink $\omega_1\cap\omega_3$: $x+12y=3$, $x+8y=0$ ⇒ **$y^*=0.75$, $x^*=-6$**. Cost $-6+0.75\cdot9.4=1.05=\sup_Q$ ✓.

**10.** *(3 points)* Suppose the call of Point 6 trades at $0.9$. The extended market ($B$, $S$ and the call) is… *(Mark the only appropriate alternative.)*
- **A.** arbitrage-free AND complete
- **B.** not arbitrage-free AND complete
- **C.** arbitrage-free AND incomplete
- **D.** not arbitrage-free AND incomplete

> **Solution.** $0.9\in(0.7,1.05)$ and $\operatorname{rank}[A|c_L]=3$ ⇒ **A**. The unique $Q$: $q_3+0.4=0.9$ ⇒ $Q=(0.2,\ 0.3,\ 0.5)$.

**11.** *(4 points)* A new European call on $S$ with strike $K_H\ge9.45$ and maturity $T=1$ is introduced in the new extended market (with the call of Point 6 priced $0.9$). Write its terminal payoff as a function of $K_H$ for $9.45\le K_H<10.5$ and for $10.5<K_H\le12.6$.

> **Solution.** For $9.45\le K_H<10.5$: $c_{K_H}(1)=(12.6-K_H,\ 10.5-K_H,\ 0)$. For $10.5<K_H\le12.6$: $c_{K_H}(1)=(12.6-K_H,\ 0,\ 0)$.

**12.** *(4 points)* Compute the initial NA price $c_{K_H}(0)$ of this call as a function of $K_H$ (in the two ranges).

> **Solution.** $c_{K_H}(0)=\frac{0.2(12.6-K_H)+0.3(10.5-K_H)}{1.05}=5.4-0.4762K_H$ for $9.45\le K_H<10.5$; $c_{K_H}(0)=\frac{0.2(12.6-K_H)}{1.05}=2.4-0.19048K_H$ for $10.5<K_H\le12.6$.

**13.** *(2 points)* If the initial NA price of the call with strike $K_H$ is $0.2$, then $K_H=$ …

> **Solution.** First branch: $K_H=10.92\notin[9.45,10.5)$ — rejected. Second branch: **$K_H=11.55$** ✓.

**14.** *(3 points)* A European call bull spread with payoff $X_{BS}(1)=(S(1)-K_L)^+-(S(1)-K_H)^+$ (with $K_H$ of Point 13) is introduced. Find its initial NA price.

> **Solution.** By linearity: $0.9-0.2=$ **0.7**.

### [Jan 16, 2023 — General] Exercise 1 (40 points)

**Text.** Consider a one-period market with the riskless asset $B$ yielding $r=5\%$, and two risky securities $S_1$ and $S_2$ whose prices at $T=1$ are
$$S_1(1)=(4.2,\ 3.15,\ 2.1),\qquad S_2(1)=(7.35,\ 4.2,\ 1.05)\qquad\text{on }(\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3).$$

**1.** *(4 points)* Is the market complete? Justify your answer.

> **Solution.** $A=\begin{pmatrix}1.05&4.2&7.35\\1.05&3.15&4.2\\1.05&2.1&1.05\end{pmatrix}$ has $\det A=0$ (indeed $S_2(1)=3S_1(1)-5.25$: $S_2$ is redundant) ⇒ $\operatorname{rank}A=2<3$ ⇒ **incomplete**.

**2.** *(7 points)* Suppose the risky securities trade at $t=0$ at $S_1(0)=2.7$ and $S_2(0)=3.1$. Determine the set of risk-neutral probabilities $Q(\omega_k)$, $k=1,2,3$.

> **Solution.** $4q_1+3q_2+2q_3=2.7$ and $7q_1+4q_2+q_3=3.1$ (prices divided by 1.05), with $q_1=1-q_2-q_3$ ⇒ $q_2+2q_3=1.3$ and $3q_2+6q_3=3.9$ (the same equation: consistent, so LOP holds) ⇒ $q_2=1.3-2q_3$, $q_1=q_3-0.3$, **$q_3\in(0.3,\ 0.65)$**.

**3.** *(3 points)* Is the market free of arbitrage opportunities? Justify.

> **Solution.** Yes: there are infinitely many RN probabilities ⇒ arbitrage-free (1st FTAP).

**4.** *(7 points)* A European *best of the two assets* option on $S_1$ and $S_2$ with maturity $T=1$ is introduced: it gives the right to choose between $S_1$ and $S_2$ at maturity, so $X(1)=\max\{S_1(1),S_2(1)\}$. Is it possible to replicate it with $B$, $S_1$ and $S_2$? Determine the set of NA prices at $t=0$.

> **Solution.** $X(1)=(7.35,\ 4.2,\ 2.1)$. $\det[B,S_1,X]=-1.16\neq0$ ⇒ rank 3 ⇒ **NO**. Price $=7q_1+4q_2+2q_3=q_3+3.1$ ⇒ **NA interval $(3.4,\ 3.75)$**.

**5.** *(8 points)* Consider strategies $\vartheta=(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1,0)$ that do not involve $S_2$. Describe the set of all $\vartheta=(x,y,0)$ whose final value super-replicates $X(1)$.

> **Solution.** $x+4y\ge7$, $x+3y\ge4$, $x+2y\ge2$ ⇒ $y\ge\max\{\frac{7-x}4,\frac{4-x}3,\frac{2-x}2\}$. Envelope: the $\omega_3$ line for $x\le-3$, the $\omega_1$ line for $x\ge-3$ (the $\omega_2$ constraint is never binding: at $x=-3$ it gives $7/3<2.5$).

**6.** *(2 points)* Among the strategies of Point 5, let $\vartheta^*=(x^*,y^*,0)$ be the one whose initial cashflow $C_\vartheta(0)$ is maximum. The riskless component $x^*$ is …

**7.** *(2 points)* The risky component $y^*$ is …

> **Solution (6–7).** Maximum $C_\vartheta(0)$ = minimum cost $x+2.7y$: slope $-1/2.7$ lies between $-\frac12$ and $-\frac14$ ⇒ optimum at the kink $\omega_1\cap\omega_3$: $x+4y=7$, $x+2y=2$ ⇒ **$x^*=-3$, $y^*=2.5$**; cost $3.75=\sup_Q$ ✓ (payoff $(7.35,\,4.725,\,2.1)\ge X$).
> **Note on the official solution:** the official sheet reports $(x,y)=(-2,2)$ with value $3.4$, obtained by intersecting the $\omega_2$ and $\omega_3$ lines; that portfolio pays $(6.3,4.2,2.1)$ and **fails** to super-replicate in $\omega_1$. $3.4$ is the *lower* NA bound; the minimum super-replication cost must equal the **upper** bound $3.75$.

**8.** *(4 points)* Suppose the best-of option trades at $3.6$. The extended market ($B$, $S_1$, $S_2$ and the option) is… *(Mark the appropriate alternatives.)*
- **A.** arbitrage-free AND complete
- **B.** not arbitrage-free AND complete
- **C.** arbitrage-free AND incomplete
- **D.** not arbitrage-free AND incomplete

> **Solution.** $3.6\in(3.4,3.75)$ and the option completes the market ⇒ **A**.

**9.** *(3 points)* A European *Margrabe* option on $S_1$ and $S_2$ with maturity $T=1$ is introduced in the extended market of Point 8: it gives the possibility of exchanging $S_2$ for $S_1$ at maturity, $X(1)=\max\{S_1(1)-S_2(1),0\}$. Find its initial NA price.

> **Solution.** $\max\{S_1-S_2,0\}=\max\{S_1,S_2\}-S_2$ ⇒ price $=3.6-3.1=$ **0.5**. (Check: unique $Q$ from $q_3+3.1=3.6$: $Q=(0.2,0.3,0.5)$; payoff $(0,0,1.05)$ ⇒ $\frac{1.05\cdot0.5}{1.05}=0.5$.)
