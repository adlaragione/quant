# Chapter 11 — No-Arbitrage Valuation in the Multi-Period Case

> **Exam-required item in this chapter:**
> - **Proposition 123** (NA valuation of a redundant derivative, multi-period) — *statement*
>
> Past exams: multiple-choice on which price processes make the extended market arbitrage-free (Jan 2022 2nd partial).

## 11.1 Framework
- **Initial market:** locally risk-free $B=S_0$ and risky $S_1,\dots,S_N$; assumed **arbitrage-free**.
- **New security:** cashflows $\{X(t)\}_{t=1}^T$, price process $\{S_X(t)\}_{t=0}^T$. At $T$ liquidation and final cashflow coincide: $X(T) = S_X(T)$ (convention: $S_X(t)$ for $t<T$ is the **ex-dividend** price, i.e. after the cashflow $X(t)$ is paid).
- **Extended market** = initial + new security.
- **Pricing problem:** find conditions on $\{S_X(t)\}$ so that the extended market remains arbitrage-free. One prices an entire **process**; the new security can be traded at intermediate dates.

## 11.2 Redundant securities
The new security is **redundant** if there is a dynamic strategy $\vartheta_X = \{\vartheta_X(t)\}_{t=0}^{T-1}$ in the initial market with $C_{\vartheta_X}(t) = X(t)$ for $t=1,\dots,T$.

> ### ★ EXAM — Proposition 123 [BOR26] (NA valuation of a redundant derivative, multi-period) — statement
> If the new security is redundant, the following are equivalent:
> 1. the extended market is arbitrage-free;
> 2. for every replicating strategy $\vartheta_X$: $S_X(t) = V_{\vartheta_X}(t)$, $t=0,1,\dots,T$;
> 3. for every risk-neutral probability $Q$ of the initial market,
> $$S_X(t) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{X(t+1)+S_X(t+1)}{1+r(t)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\right],\qquad t=0,\dots,T-2,$$
> and
> $$S_X(T-1) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{X(T)}{1+r(T-1)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_{T-1}\right].$$

- **Replication (pricing by hedging):** $S_X(t) = V_{\vartheta_X}(t)$ — gives price process **and** hedge.
- **Risk-neutral recursion:** current price = conditional expected discounted value of **next cashflow + next ex-dividend price**. At $T-1$ only the final cashflow remains.

**Discounted form:** since $B(t+1)=B(t)(1+r(t))$,
$$\frac{S_X(t)}{B(t)} = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{X(t+1)+S_X(t+1)}{B(t+1)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\right],\qquad \frac{S_X(T-1)}{B(T-1)} = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{X(T)}{B(T)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_{T-1}\right].$$

## 11.3 Closed form (Gordon's formula)
The recursion is equivalent to
$$\boxed{S_X(t) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\sum_{\tau=t+1}^T\frac{B(t)}{B(\tau)}X(\tau)\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\right],\qquad t=0,\dots,T-1.}$$
Price at $t$ = conditional expectation of all **future** cashflows discounted back to $t$. Discount factor over $[t,\tau[$: $\frac{B(t)}{B(\tau)} = \prod_{s=t}^{\tau-1}(1+r(s))^{-1}$.

For a **European** claim (only $X(T)\neq0$): $S_X(t) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{B(t)}{B(T)}X(T)\mid\mathcal{P}_t\right]$, so $\tilde S_X(t) = S_X(t)/B(t)$ is a $Q$-martingale.

**Exam technique — backward induction on the tree:**
1. Write terminal payoffs $X(T)(\omega_k)$.
2. At each node at $T-1$: $S_X(T-1) = \frac{1}{1+r(T-1)}\sum Q[\omega\mid f]\,X(T)(\omega)$.
3. Move back: $S_X(t) = \frac{1}{1+r(t)}\sum Q[\text{succ}\mid f]\,(X(t+1)+S_X(t+1))$.
4. Alternatively at $t=0$ directly: $S_X(0) = \sum_k Q[\omega_k]\frac{X(T)(\omega_k)}{B(T)(\omega_k)}$ (careful: with stochastic rates $B(T)$ differs across paths!).

**Hedging strategy by backward recursion:** at each node, solve the local replication system: $\vartheta_0(t)B(t+1) + \vartheta_1(t)S(t+1) = X(t+1)+S_X(t+1)$ in each successor. For a binomial node:
$$\vartheta_1(t) = \frac{V_u - V_d}{S_u - S_d}, \qquad \vartheta_0(t) = \frac{V_u - \vartheta_1(t)S_u}{B(t+1)}$$
(where $V_u, V_d$ are the values to be replicated at the up/down successor; remember $B(t+1)$, not $1+r$, multiplies $\vartheta_0$ when $\vartheta_0$ is in units of $B$).

## 11.4 Consequences for redundant securities
- Unique NA price process, **independent of which EMM** is used.
- If the initial market is **dynamically complete**, every new claim is redundant.

## 11.5 Non-redundant securities
If the new security cannot be replicated, its NA price process is **not unique**: arbitrage-free pricing gives a **range** (as in one period, via super-replication / extremal EMM expectations).

## 11.6 Path-dependent derivatives (frequent in exams)
Because scenarios are whole paths, payoffs can depend on the path:
- **Asian option:** payoff depends on the average $A(T) = \frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^T S(t)$ (arithmetic average, check the exam's definition: may include $S(0)$ or not). Asian call: $(A(T)-K)^+$; Asian put: $(K-A(T))^+$; average-strike: $(S(T)-A(T))^+$.
- **Lookback:** payoff depends on $\max_t S(t)$ or $\min_t S(t)$, e.g. $\max_t S(t) - S(T)$.
- **Barrier options:** knock-out (pays only if barrier $b$ is never touched), knock-in (pays only if touched). Up-and-out: $\max_t S(t) < b$; down-and-out: $\min_t S(t) > b$.
- Price exactly as above: compute payoff scenario by scenario (path by path), then backward induction.
- Also: **forward-start** options, **chooser** options, derivatives paying at intermediate dates (use Gordon's formula with intermediate cashflows), **American** options (compare continuation value vs immediate exercise at each node — only if asked).

## 11.7 Takeaways
Pricing concerns an entire process; for a redundant security the NA price process is unique and equals both the replicating value process and the RN discounted value of future cashflows; for a non-redundant security NA no longer singles out a unique price process.

**Question.** Let $Q$ denote any risk-neutral probability for an arbitrage-free discrete multiperiod market. Consider the redundant cashflow $X=\{X(t)\}_{t=1}^T$ and let $S_X=\{S_X(t)\}_{t=0}^T$ be its price process. The extended market is arbitrage free if and only if…

- **A.** $S_X(t)=V_{\vartheta^X}(t)$ for all $t=0,\dots,T$ and for every replicating strategy $\vartheta^X$
- **B.** $S_X(t)=\sum_{\tau=t+1}^T\mathbb{E}^Q\Big[\frac{B(t)}{B(\tau)}X(\tau)\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\Big]$ for all $t=0,\dots,T-1$
- **C.** $\begin{cases}S_X(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q\Big[\frac{X(t+1)}{1+r(t)}\Big|\mathcal{P}_t\Big] & t=0,\dots,T-2\\[4pt] S_X(T-1)=\mathbb{E}^Q\Big[\frac{X(T)}{1+r(T-1)}\Big|\mathcal{P}_{T-1}\Big] & t=T-1\end{cases}$
- **D.** $\begin{cases}S_X(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q\Big[\frac{X(t+1)+S_X(t+1)}{1+r(t)}\Big|\mathcal{P}_t\Big] & t=0,\dots,T-2\\[4pt] S_X(T-1)=\mathbb{E}^Q\Big[\frac{X(T)}{1+r(T-1)}\Big|\mathcal{P}_{T-1}\Big] & t=T-1\end{cases}$

**Correct answers: A, B, D.**
- A = replication (Prop. 123, item 2); D = risk-neutral recursion (Prop. 123, item 3); B = Gordon's formula, equivalent to D.
- C is wrong: it forgets the next-period ex-dividend price $S_X(t+1)$.
