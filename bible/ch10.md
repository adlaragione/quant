# Chapter 10 — Dynamically Complete Multi-Period Markets

> No items of this chapter are on the official 2026–27 list, but the **multi-period 2nd FTAP** has been asked in past exams ("State the Second FTAP in the multiperiod market model", Jan 2022 2nd partial) and completeness questions appear in every multi-period exercise ("Is the market arbitrage-free and complete? Justify").

## 10.1 Overview
In multi-period markets completeness means replicating **entire future cashflow streams** through **dynamic trading and rebalancing**. Local one-period completeness is key. Main conclusion: **NA + dynamic completeness ⟺ unique martingale measure.**

## 10.2 Multi-period contingent claims
A **contingent claim** is any **adapted** sequence $X=\{X(t)\}_{t=1}^T$, where $X(t):\mathcal{P}_t\to\mathbb{R}$ is the cashflow delivered at $t$. A claim may pay at several dates ⇒ replication must match the whole future cashflow process.

## 10.3 Attainability
$X$ is **attainable** if there is a dynamic strategy $\vartheta$ with
$$C_\vartheta(t) = X(t),\qquad t=1,\dots,T,$$
node by node, date by date. (For a European claim paying only at $T$: $C_\vartheta(t)=0$ for $t<T$, i.e. **self-financing**, and $V_\vartheta(T)=X(T)$.)

## 10.4 Dynamic completeness — Definition 116 [BOR26]
A discrete-time multi-period market is **dynamically complete** if every contingent claim $X=\{X(t)\}_{t=1}^T$ is attainable. "Dynamically" stresses intermediate rebalancing.

**Why stronger than static:** in one period completeness is pure static spanning; in multi-period, investors update positions after observing information ⇒ rebalancing **enlarges** the set of attainable cashflows. Intermediate dates act as extra replication opportunities.

## 10.5 Characterization — Proposition 117 [BOR26]
- A multi-period market is dynamically complete **if every one-period submarket is complete**.
- Conversely, if it is dynamically complete **and every one-period submarket satisfies the LOP**, then every one-period submarket is complete.

**Remark 118:** dynamic completeness alone does not imply local completeness; the LOP is essential in the converse.

**Practical test (exam):** at each non-terminal node, the local payoff matrix (rows = successor nodes, columns = $B$ and risky assets, values = next-period prices) must have **rank = number of successors**. In a binomial tree with one risky asset and $u\ne d$ at every node ⇒ dynamically complete. Combined with 2nd FTAP: unique local $Q$ at every node ⟺ complete (given NA).

Sufficiency via **backward recursion**: replicate from the last step $T-1\to T$ (solve local system at each node at $T-1$), then move backward: at $t$ replicate the claim $X(t+1)+V_\vartheta(t+1)$ in each subtree, until $t=0$.

## 10.6 Example: re-trading completes the market
Two-period tree, $r(0)=r(1)=5\%$:
```
t=0          t=1                 t=2
                          ω1: S(2)=15.6
          f11: S(1)=12 ──┤
S(0)=10 ──┤               ω2: S(2)=8.4
                          ω3: S(2)=9.6
          f21: S(1)=8  ──┤
                          ω4: S(2)=6.4
```
- With trading only at $t=0$ (a one-period market with 4 states), completeness would require **4** linearly independent securities.
- With re-trading at $t=1$ (investors observe $f^1_1$ or $f^1_2$ and rebalance), **2 securities ($B$ and $S$) suffice**: each one-period subtree is a binomial model.
- Check: node $f^1_1$: $u=1.3$, $d=0.7$; $q=(1.05-0.7)/0.6 = 0.5833$. Node $f^1_2$: $u=1.2$, $d=0.8$, $q=(1.05-0.8)/0.4=0.625$. Root: $u=1.2$, $d=0.8$, $q=0.625$. All in $(0,1)$ ⇒ NA and complete.

**Main message:** dynamic trading can complete a market that is incomplete in a purely one-period (static) representation.

## 10.7 Second FTAP (multi-period) — Theorem 122 [BOR26]
In a discrete-time multi-period market the following are equivalent:
1. the market is both **arbitrage-free** and **dynamically complete**;
2. there exists **one and only one** martingale measure.

NA ⇒ existence; dynamic completeness ⇒ uniqueness ⇒ unique NA valuation of contingent claims.

## 10.8 Takeaways
- Multi-period completeness = spanning of cashflow **processes**.
- Rebalancing is why dynamic completeness differs from one-period completeness.
- Local completeness of all subtrees ⇒ dynamic completeness.
- NA + dynamic completeness ⟺ unique EMM.

## Past exam questions — Chapter 10

### Theory questions

**[Jan 14, 2022 – 2nd Partial, Q1 (6 pts)]** *State the Second Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing in the multiperiod market model.*
**Answer.** In a discrete-time multi-period financial market the following are equivalent: (1) the market is both arbitrage-free and dynamically complete; (2) there exists one and only one equivalent martingale measure (risk-neutral probability) $Q$. (Theorem 122.)

**[Feb 1, 2023 – 2nd Partial, Q3 (4 pts)]** *The multiperiod discrete market is dynamically complete and arbitrage-free if and only if…* A. there exists a unique RN probability — B. there exists at least one RN probability — C. there are no arbitrage opportunities and any cashflow can be dynamically replicated — D. there are no RN probabilities.
**Answer: A, C.** A is the 2nd FTAP (Thm 122); C is just the definition (NA + every claim attainable). B characterizes NA only (1st FTAP); D means arbitrage.
