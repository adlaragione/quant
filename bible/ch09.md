## 9.1 Objective
Multi-period analogue of the RN probability = **equivalent martingale measure (EMM)**. Relate it to discounted prices and gains; prove the multi-period **1st FTAP**.

## 9.2 Equivalent martingale measure

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 109 [BOR26] (Risk-neutral probability / Equivalent Martingale Measure)
> A **strictly positive** probability measure $Q$ on the terminal scenarios ($Q[\omega_k]>0$ for all $k$) is an **equivalent martingale measure** (risk-neutral probability) if, for every risky asset $j=1,\dots,N$,
> $$S_j(t) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{S_j(t+1)}{1+r(t)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\right],\qquad t=0,1,\dots,T-1.$$

Under $Q$, one-step discounted expected prices equal current prices — at every node.

**Discounted prices are martingales.** With $\tilde S_j(t) = S_j(t)/B(t)$ and $B(t+1) = B(t)(1+r(t))$ ($B(t+1)$ is $\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable, so it can be taken out):
$$\tilde S_j(t) = \mathbb{E}^Q[\tilde S_j(t+1)\mid\mathcal{P}_t],\qquad t=0,\dots,T-1,$$
so (Prop. 93) **under an EMM, discounted risky prices are $Q$-martingales.**

**Economic meaning.** Since $S_j(t)$ and $r(t)$ are $\mathcal{P}_t$-measurable:
$$\mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{S_j(t+1)-S_j(t)}{S_j(t)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\right] = r(t):$$
under $Q$ the conditional expected return of every risky asset equals the locally risk-free rate (dynamic counterpart of one-period risk-neutrality).

## 9.3 Three equivalent characterizations

> ### ★ EXAM — Remark 110 [BOR26] (RN probability and strategies)
> For a strictly positive probability $Q$, the following are equivalent:
> 1. $Q$ is an equivalent martingale measure;
> 2. for every dynamic strategy $\vartheta$,
> $$V_\vartheta(t) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{V_\vartheta(t+1)+C_\vartheta(t+1)}{1+r(t)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\right],\quad t=0,\dots,T-2,$$
> $$V_\vartheta(T-1) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{C_\vartheta(T)}{1+r(T-1)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_{T-1}\right];$$
> 3. for every dynamic strategy $\vartheta$, the discounted gain process $\tilde G_\vartheta$ is a martingale under $Q$.

Three views: **asset pricing** (discounted prices are martingales); **portfolio valuation** (current value = discounted conditional expectation of next value + next cashflow); **gains** (every discounted gain process is a $Q$-martingale).

*Why 1 ⇒ 2:* at $t+1<T$, $V_\vartheta(t+1)+C_\vartheta(t+1) = \vartheta_0(t)B(t+1)+\sum_j\vartheta_j(t)S_j(t+1)$ (liquidation value of the time-$t$ portfolio); divide by $1+r(t)$, take $\mathbb{E}^Q[\cdot\mid\mathcal{P}_t]$, take out the known $\vartheta_j(t)$ and use the EMM property ⇒ $\vartheta_0(t)B(t)+\sum_j\vartheta_j(t)S_j(t) = V_\vartheta(t)$. Same at $T-1$ with $C_\vartheta(T)=V_\vartheta(T)$.

## 9.4 Multi-period First FTAP — Theorem 111 [BOR26]
In a discrete-time multi-period market, the following are equivalent:
1. the market is **arbitrage-free**;
2. there exists an **equivalent martingale measure** $Q$.

### Proof idea NA ⇒ EMM
- If the multi-period market is arbitrage-free then **every one-period submarket (subtree) is arbitrage-free**.
- By the one-period FFTAP, each subtree (node $f^t_h$ and its successors) has a **local RN probability** $Q_{t,h}[f^{t+1}_{l}]$ (conditional probabilities).
- Define the **global** probability of a terminal state as the **product of local probabilities along the unique path** to it:
$$Q^*[\omega_k] = \prod_{t=0}^{T-1} Q_{t,h}[f^{t+1}_{l}] \quad(\text{path of } \omega_k).$$
- It is strictly positive, sums to 1, and its conditional probabilities coincide with the local ones ⇒ one-step discounted price relation holds at every node ⇒ discounted prices are martingales.

### Proof idea EMM ⇒ NA
Suppose $Q$ is an EMM and $\vartheta$ an arbitrage. All cashflows are $\ge0$ (including $C_\vartheta(0)=-V_\vartheta(0)\ge0$) and at least one is $>0$ (with positive $Q$-probability since $Q\gg0$) ⇒ $\mathbb{E}^Q[\tilde G_\vartheta(T)] = \mathbb{E}^Q[\sum_\tau C_\vartheta(\tau)/B(\tau)] > 0$. But $\tilde G_\vartheta$ is a $Q$-martingale with $\tilde G_\vartheta(0) = V_\vartheta(0) + C_\vartheta(0) = 0$ ⇒ $\mathbb{E}^Q[\tilde G_\vartheta(T)] = 0$. Contradiction. ∎

## 9.5 Operational takeaway (how to solve exam exercises)
A discrete-time market is arbitrage-free ⟺ there is $Q\gg0$ with $S_j(t)/B(t)$ a $Q$-martingale for all $j$.
**Algorithm on a tree:**
1. At each non-terminal node $f^t_h$ solve the one-period system
   $$S(t)(f^t_h) = \frac{1}{1+r(t)(f^t_h)}\sum_{l}Q[f^{t+1}_l\mid f^t_h]\,S(t+1)(f^{t+1}_l),\qquad \sum_l Q[f^{t+1}_l\mid f^t_h]=1.$$
   For a binomial node: $q_u = \frac{(1+r)-d}{u-d}$ with $u=S_{\text{up}}/S$, $d=S_{\text{down}}/S$, $r=r(t)(f^t_h)$.
2. Check strict positivity at **every node** (NA ⟺ every local system has a strictly positive solution).
3. Global: $Q[\omega_k]$ = product of conditional probabilities along the path; $Q[f^1_h]$ etc.
4. Uniqueness at every node ⟺ dynamic completeness (Ch. 10).

**Pricing viewpoint:** discount first, then take conditional expectations under $Q$. **Dynamic viewpoint:** discounted gains have zero expected drift under $Q$.

## 9.6 Key takeaways
- Multi-period analogue of RN probability = EMM.
- Under an EMM: discounted prices and discounted gains are martingales; valuation is recursive via conditional expectations.
- **1st FTAP (multi-period): NA ⟺ ∃ EMM.**

#### [Jan 16, 2023 2nd Partial — Question 1] (6 points, open question)
**Question.** State the First Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing in the multiperiod market model.

**Answer.** In a discrete-time multi-period financial market the following are equivalent:
1. the market is arbitrage-free;
2. there exists an equivalent martingale measure (risk-neutral probability) $Q$, i.e. a strictly positive probability on $\Omega$ such that $S_j(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q\big[\frac{S_j(t+1)}{1+r(t)}\,\big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\big]$ for all $j=1,\dots,N$ and $t=0,\dots,T-1$.

(Theorem 111.)

#### [Jan 2024 1st exam — Question 2] (6 points, mark all the appropriate sentences)
**Question.** Let $Q$ denote a risk-neutral probability for an arbitrage-free discrete multiperiod market with assets $B,S_1,\dots,S_N$. Then

- **A.** $Q(\omega_k)>0$ for all $\omega_k\in\Omega$
- **B.** $\{S_j(t)\}_t$ is a $Q$-martingale, for all $j=1,\dots,N$
- **C.** $S_j(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q_t\Big[\frac{S_j(t+1)}{1+r(t)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\Big]$ for all $t=0,\dots,T-1$, for all $j=1,\dots,N$
- **D.** $\{B(t)\}_t$ is a $Q$-martingale

**Correct answers: A, C** (Definition 109).
- B is false: only *discounted* prices $S_j/B$ are martingales (unless $r=0$).
- D is false: $B(t+1)=B(t)(1+r(t))>B(t)$ when $r>0$.

#### [Jan 13, 2025 — Question 2] (2 points)
**Question.** Let $Q$ denote a risk-neutral probability for an arbitrage-free discrete multiperiod market with assets $B,S_1,\dots,S_N$. Then

- **A.** $Q(\omega_k)=0$ for all $\omega_k\in\Omega$
- **B.** $S_j(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q\Big[\frac{S_j(t+1)}{1+r(t+1)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\Big]$ for all $t=0,\dots,T-1$, for all $j$
- **C.** $\{S_j(t)\}_{t=0}^T$ is a $Q$-martingale, for all $j=1,\dots,N$
- **D.** $S_j(t)=\mathbb{E}^Q\Big[\frac{S_j(t+1)}{1+r(t)}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{P}_t\Big]$ for all $t=0,\dots,T-1$, for all $j$

**Correct answer: D.**
- A: $Q$ must be strictly positive.
- B: wrong rate — over $[t,t+1]$ the rate is $r(t)$, known at $t$, not $r(t+1)$.
- C: only discounted prices are martingales.
