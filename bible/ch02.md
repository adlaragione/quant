## 2.1 Overview
NA in a one-period market is characterized by the existence of a **risk-neutral probability**, which prices every traded asset by **discounted expectation**. Equivalent formulations: **state-price vectors** (Arrow–Debreu prices) and (in the book) stochastic discount factors.

## 2.2 Risk-neutral probability

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 20 [BOR26] (Risk-neutral probability)
> In a one-period financial market, a probability $Q$ on $\Omega = \{\omega_1,\dots,\omega_K\}$ is a **risk-neutral probability** if:
> 1. $Q[\omega_k] > 0$ for all $k=1,\dots,K$;
> 2. $\sum_{k=1}^K Q[\omega_k] = 1$;
> 3. for every risky asset $j=1,\dots,N$:
> $$S_j(0) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{S_j(1)}{1+r}\right] = \frac{1}{1+r}\sum_{k=1}^K Q[\omega_k]\,S_j(1)(\omega_k).$$

Remarks:
- **Strict positivity** is essential: $Q$ must be *equivalent* to $P$ (same null sets; here: no state has zero probability). A "probability" with some $Q[\omega_k]=0$ solving the pricing equations is **not** a risk-neutral probability (and signals an arbitrage).
- Condition 3 for $j=0$ (the bond) is automatic: $\mathbb{E}^Q[(1+r)/(1+r)] = 1 = B(0)$.
- **How to compute $Q$ in exercises:** solve the linear system
$$\begin{cases} \sum_k q_k = 1\\ \sum_k q_k S_j(1)(\omega_k) = (1+r)S_j(0), \quad j=1,\dots,N\end{cases}$$
  and then impose $q_k > 0$. If the solution is unique and strictly positive ⇒ NA + completeness. If there is a family of solutions (free parameters) ⇒ intersect with $q_k>0$ to get the set of all RN probabilities (incomplete market). If no strictly positive solution exists ⇒ arbitrage.

## 2.3 Economic meaning of risk-neutrality
Rewrite condition 3:
$$\mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{S_j(1)-S_j(0)}{S_j(0)}\right] = r, \qquad j=1,\dots,N.$$
Under $Q$ **every risky asset has expected return equal to the risk-free rate** — assets are priced *as if* investors were risk-neutral. In general $Q[\omega_k] \neq P[\omega_k]$: $Q$ is a pricing device, not a forecast. Under $P$ risky assets typically earn a **risk premium** $\mathbb{E}^P[R_j] - r > 0$.

## 2.4 Pricing of strategies — Proposition 21 [BOR26]
The following are equivalent:
1. $Q$ is a risk-neutral probability;
2. for every strategy $\vartheta$: $\displaystyle V_\vartheta(0) = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{V_\vartheta(1)}{1+r}\right]$.

*Proof sketch.* (1⇒2) by linearity of expectation: $\mathbb{E}^Q[V_\vartheta(1)/(1+r)] = \vartheta_0 + \sum_j \vartheta_j \mathbb{E}^Q[S_j(1)/(1+r)] = \vartheta_0+\sum_j\vartheta_j S_j(0)$. (2⇒1) apply 2 to the strategy holding one unit of asset $j$ only. ∎

## 2.5 Discounted prices are martingales (one-period version)
With $B(0)=1$, $B(1)=1+r$:
$$\frac{S_j(0)}{B(0)} = \mathbb{E}^Q\!\left[\frac{S_j(1)}{B(1)}\right], \quad j=1,\dots,N.$$
Under $Q$, **discounted prices** $\tilde S_j(t) = S_j(t)/B(t)$ are (one-step) **martingales**. This is the seed of the multi-period theory (Ch. 9) and of continuous time (Ch. 15). For this reason $Q$ is also called an **equivalent martingale measure (EMM)**.

## 2.6 First Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing

> ### ★ EXAM — Theorem 27 [BOR26] (1st FTAP, one period) — statement
> In a one-period financial market, the following are equivalent:
> 1. **no-arbitrage holds**;
> 2. **there exists a risk-neutral probability** $Q$.

- NA and existence of RN probability are two views of the same condition. Checking NA reduces to checking existence of a **strictly positive** pricing probability.
- The direction (2 ⇒ 1) is easy (and worth knowing): if $Q$ exists and $\vartheta$ has $V_\vartheta(1)\ge0$ with $V_\vartheta(1)(\omega_{\bar k})>0$, then $V_\vartheta(0)=\mathbb{E}^Q[V_\vartheta(1)]/(1+r) \ge Q[\omega_{\bar k}]V_\vartheta(1)(\omega_{\bar k})/(1+r)>0$ (since $Q[\omega_{\bar k}]>0$) — violating (1); and if $V_\vartheta(1)\ge0$ then $V_\vartheta(0)\ge0$, so (3II) is impossible. Hence no arbitrage.

## 2.7 Cashflow matrix — Definition 28 [BOR26]
The $(K+1)\times(N+1)$ **cashflow matrix**:
$$M = \begin{pmatrix}
-1 & -S_1(0) & \cdots & -S_N(0)\\
1+r & S_1(1)(\omega_1) & \cdots & S_N(1)(\omega_1)\\
\vdots & \vdots & & \vdots\\
1+r & S_1(1)(\omega_K) & \cdots & S_N(1)(\omega_K)
\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\text{(time-0 prices)} \\ A \end{pmatrix}.$$
For any strategy: $M\vartheta = \big(-V_\vartheta(0),\, V_\vartheta(1)(\omega_1),\dots,V_\vartheta(1)(\omega_K)\big)^T$, i.e. the cashflow vector $C_\vartheta$.

## 2.8 Linear characterization of NA — Proposition 29 [BOR26]
The market satisfies NA **iff there is no strategy $\vartheta$ with $M\vartheta > 0$** (where $x>0$ means $x\ge0$ componentwise and $x\neq 0$).

## 2.9 Stiemke's Lemma — Lemma 30 [BOR26]
Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times l}$. **Exactly one** of the following is true:
1. there exists $y \gg 0$ (all components strictly positive) with $y^T X = 0$;
2. there exists $x$ with $Xx > 0$.

(Theorem of the alternative: turns a system of inequalities into a system of equalities. Key linear-algebra tool behind the 1st FTAP.)

## 2.10 Proof idea of the 1st FTAP
NA ⟺ (Prop. 29) no $\vartheta$ with $M\vartheta>0$ ⟺ (Stiemke) there is $y=(y_0,y_1,\dots,y_K)\gg0$ with $y^TM=0$, i.e.
- column 0: $-y_0 + (1+r)\sum_k y_k = 0$;
- column $j$: $-y_0 S_j(0) + \sum_k y_k S_j(1)(\omega_k)=0$.
Normalize: $Q[\omega_k] = (1+r)\,y_k/y_0 > 0$. Column 0 gives $\sum_k Q[\omega_k] = 1$; column $j$ gives $S_j(0) = \sum_k \frac{Q[\omega_k]}{1+r} S_j(1)(\omega_k)$. ∎

## 2.11 State-price vectors — Definition 31 [BOR26]
A vector $\psi\in\mathbb{R}^K$ is a **state-price vector** if $\psi(\omega_k) > 0$ for all $k$ and
$$\sum_{k=1}^K \psi(\omega_k) = \frac{1}{1+r}, \qquad S_j(0) = \sum_{k=1}^K \psi(\omega_k) S_j(1)(\omega_k), \quad j=1,\dots,N.$$
- $\psi(\omega_k)$ = time-0 price of **one unit of money paid at $t=1$ iff state $\omega_k$ occurs** = **Arrow–Debreu state price**.
- Current prices are linear combinations of state-contingent payoffs weighted by state prices.
- **One-to-one link with $Q$:** $\psi(\omega_k) = \dfrac{Q[\omega_k]}{1+r}$, $Q[\omega_k] = (1+r)\psi(\omega_k)$.
- Hence NA ⟺ ∃ RN probability ⟺ ∃ strictly positive state-price vector.

## 2.12 (Supplement) Stochastic discount factor
Not on slides but in the book: $m(\omega_k) = \psi(\omega_k)/P[\omega_k] = \frac{Q[\omega_k]}{(1+r)P[\omega_k]}$ is the **stochastic discount factor (SDF)/pricing kernel**: $S_j(0) = \mathbb{E}^P[m\,S_j(1)]$. NA ⟺ existence of a strictly positive SDF. The ratio $L = dQ/dP = Q[\omega_k]/P[\omega_k]$ is the **Radon–Nikodym density** (it appears again in Girsanov, Ch. 15).

## 2.13 Key takeaways
- NA ⟺ existence of a strictly positive pricing rule.
- That rule can be expressed as (i) a risk-neutral probability $Q$; (ii) a state-price vector $\psi$; (iii) an SDF $m$. All equivalent.
- Under $Q$: expected return of every asset $= r$; discounted prices are martingales.
