## 14.1 Overview
Processes indexed by $t\in[0,T]$. Information evolves through a **filtration** of sigma-algebras (instead of partitions). Topics: measurability, filtrations, adapted processes; Wiener process; Itô integral and Itô processes; Itô's formula and diffusions.

## 14.2 Sigma-algebras — Definition 135 [BOR26]
A **sigma-algebra** $\Sigma$ on $\Omega$ is a collection of subsets with: (1) $\emptyset\in\Sigma$; (2) $A\in\Sigma\Rightarrow A^c\in\Sigma$; (3) $A_n\in\Sigma\ \forall n \Rightarrow \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\in\Sigma$. Closed under complements and countable unions (hence countable intersections). Generalizes partitions (a finite partition generates the sigma-algebra of all unions of its cells). $\Sigma'\supseteq\Sigma$ ⇒ $\Sigma'$ contains at least as much information.

## 14.3 Measurability — Definition 136 and Definition 137 [BOR26]
- $Y:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ is **$\Sigma$-measurable** if $Y^{-1}((a,b))\in\Sigma$ for every open interval $(a,b)$ (you can tell, with info $\Sigma$, whether $Y$ fell in any interval ⇒ $Y$ is known).
- $\Sigma'$ is **finer** than $\Sigma$ if every set of $\Sigma$ belongs to $\Sigma'$.

## 14.4 Filtrations and adapted processes — Definition 138 and Definition 139 [BOR26]
- **Filtration** $\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_t\}_{t\in[0,T]}$: increasingly finer sigma-algebras with $\mathcal{F}_0=\{\emptyset,\Omega\}$, $\mathcal{F}_T=\mathcal{F}$, each $\mathcal{F}_t$ containing all null-probability events.
- $X=\{X(t)\}$ is **adapted** to $\mathbb{F}$ if $X(t)$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable for every $t$ (its value is known at $t$).
- (In continuous time **predictability** matters when paths can jump.)

## 14.5 Conditional expectation and independence
$\mathbb{E}^P[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_t]$ = best forecast of integrable $Y$ given info at $t$. If $Y$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable, $\mathbb{E}^P[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_t]=Y$. Tower property, taking out what is known, linearity hold as in discrete time.
**Proposition 140:** if $Y$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_t$, then $\mathbb{E}^P[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_t] = \mathbb{E}^P[Y]$.

## 14.6 Continuous vs jump trajectories
Processes can have **continuous paths** (Fig. 1 on slides: a rough but unbroken path) or **jumps** (Fig. 2: path with discontinuities). **The course focuses on continuous-path processes.**

## 14.7 Wiener process

> ### ★ EXAM — Definition 141 [BOR26] (Standard Brownian motion / Wiener process)
> A process $W=\{W(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}$, adapted to $\mathbb{F}$, is a **standard Brownian motion under $P$** if:
> 1. $W(0)=0$;
> 2. $t\mapsto W(t)(\omega)$ is **continuous** (almost surely);
> 3. for any $0=t_0<t_1<\dots<t_n\le T$ the increments $W(t_1)-W(t_0),\dots,W(t_n)-W(t_{n-1})$ are **mutually independent**;
> 4. for all $0\le s<t\le T$: $W(t)-W(s)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\,t-s)$.

**Key properties:**
- $W(t)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,t)$: variance grows **linearly** in time; st. dev. $\sqrt{t}$.
- Increments are **independent** and **stationary** (law depends only on interval length).
- Paths are continuous but **nowhere differentiable**, with unbounded (first) variation; **quadratic variation** over $[0,t]$ equals $t$ ⇒ heuristic rules $(dW)^2 = dt$, $dW\,dt = 0$, $(dt)^2=0$.
- $\mathbb{E}[W(t)W(s)] = \min(s,t)$; $\mathbb{E}[W(t)^2]=t$; $\mathbb{E}[W(t)^4] = 3t^2$; $\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma W(t)}] = e^{\sigma^2t/2}$ (moment generating function of a normal: if $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(m,v)$ then $\mathbb{E}[e^Z]=e^{m+v/2}$).
- Standardization: $W(t) = \sqrt t\,Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$.

**Generalized Brownian motion — Definition 143:** $B_t = mt+\sqrt{v}\,W_t$ ⇒ $B_t-B_s\sim\mathcal{N}(m(t-s),\,v(t-s))$.

**Proposition 144:** $W$ is a **martingale** under $P$ w.r.t. its own filtration: for $s<t$, $\mathbb{E}^P[W(t)\mid\mathcal{F}_s] = W(s)+\mathbb{E}^P[W(t)-W(s)] = W(s)$ (increment independent of $\mathcal{F}_s$ with mean zero).
Other standard martingales: $W(t)^2 - t$; $e^{\sigma W(t) - \frac12\sigma^2t}$ (exponential martingale). Not a martingale: $W(t)^2$ (submartingale), $e^{\sigma W(t)}$.

In finance the filtration is typically the one **generated by $W$**: observing prices ⟺ observing the source of randomness.

## 14.8 From returns to dynamics
Continuously compounded return: $\eta(t) = \ln\frac{S(t)}{S(0)}$. Increment decomposition:
$$\Delta X(t) = \alpha(t)\Delta t + \beta(t)\Delta W(t),$$
$\alpha(t)\Delta t$ = predictable component (local conditional mean), $\beta(t)\Delta W(t)$ = unpredictable shock (local conditional variance). In Black–Scholes $\alpha,\beta$ are constant.

**Lemma 145 (conditional moments of increments):**
$$\mathbb{E}_t[\Delta X(t)] = \alpha(t)\Delta t,\quad \mathbb{E}_t[(\Delta X(t))^2] = (\alpha(t)\Delta t)^2+\beta^2(t)\Delta t,\quad \operatorname{Var}_t[\Delta X(t)] = \beta^2(t)\Delta t.$$
$\alpha$ = conditional expected rate; $\beta$ = instantaneous magnitude of uncertainty (note $(\alpha\Delta t)^2$ is negligible vs $\beta^2\Delta t$ as $\Delta t\to0$).

## 14.9 Stochastic integration (construction)
From increments to levels: if $\beta=0$, $X(T)-X(0)=\int_0^T\alpha(t)\,dt$. If $\beta\ne0$ we must aggregate Brownian shocks: $\int_0^\tau\beta(t)\,dW(t)$. Path-by-path integration fails: $\lim_{\Delta t\to0}\Delta W/\Delta t$ does **not** exist (nowhere differentiable, unbounded variation).

- **Step 1 — constant $\beta$:** $X(T)-X(0) = \beta W(T) \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\beta^2T)$.
- **Step 2 — piecewise constant deterministic:** $\beta_0$ on $[0,t_1)$, $\beta_1$ on $[t_1,T]$: $X(T)-X(0) = \beta_0W(t_1)+\beta_1(W(T)-W(t_1))\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\beta_0^2t_1+\beta_1^2(T-t_1))$ (independent increments).
- **Step 3 — random but predictable:** $\beta(t)=\beta(t_1)$ on $[t_1,T]$ with $\beta(t_1)$ $\mathcal{F}_{t_1}$-measurable (may depend on info at $t_1$, **not** on future increments): $X(T)-X(0)=\beta_0W(t_1)+\beta(t_1)(W(T)-W(t_1))$.
  - Mean: $0$ (since $\mathbb{E}_{t_1}[W(T)-W(t_1)]=0$).
  - Second moment: $\mathbb{E}[\beta_0^2]t_1 + \mathbb{E}[\beta^2(t_1)](T-t_1) = \int_0^T\mathbb{E}[\beta^2(t)]\,dt$ — the area below the step function $t\mapsto\mathbb{E}[\beta^2(t)]$ (slide figure). (Generally **not** normal any more.)
- **Towards the Itô integral:** start with simple (piecewise constant, adapted) processes, define the integral as the weighted sum of Brownian increments with weights fixed at the **beginning** of each interval, extend by approximation.

## 14.10 Properties of the Itô integral — Proposition 148 [BOR26]
If $\beta$ is adapted with $\int_0^T\mathbb{E}[\beta^2(t)]\,dt<\infty$, then for every $\tau\in[0,T]$:
1. $\int_0^\tau\beta(t)\,dW(t)$ is $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable;
2. $\mathbb{E}\big[\int_0^\tau\beta(t)\,dW(t)\big]=0$;
3. **Itô isometry:** $\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\int_0^\tau\beta(t)\,dW(t)\big)^2\Big] = \int_0^\tau\mathbb{E}[\beta^2(t)]\,dt$;
4. $\big\{\int_0^\tau\beta(t)\,dW(t)\big\}_{\tau\in[0,T]}$ is a **martingale**.

Also: if $\beta$ is **deterministic**, $\int_0^\tau\beta(t)\,dW(t)\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\int_0^\tau\beta^2(t)\,dt\big)$.

## 14.11 Itô processes — Definition 149 [BOR26]
$X$ is an **Itô process** if
$$dX(t)=\alpha(t)\,dt+\beta(t)\,dW(t)\iff X(t)=x_0+\int_0^t\alpha(s)\,ds+\int_0^t\beta(s)\,dW(s),$$
$\alpha,\beta$ adapted and integrable. First integral = cumulative predictable drift; second = cumulative random shocks.
- $\mathbb{E}[X(t)] = x_0 + \int_0^t\mathbb{E}[\alpha(s)]ds$. An Itô process is a martingale iff its drift is zero (under integrability).

## 14.12 Itô's formula

> ### ★ EXAM — Lemma 150 [BOR26] (Itô's formula) — statement
> Let $dX(t)=\alpha(t)\,dt+\beta(t)\,dW(t)$ and let $\varphi:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuously differentiable w.r.t. $t$ and twice continuously differentiable w.r.t. $x$. Define $Y(t)=\varphi(t,X(t))$. Then $Y$ is also an Itô process and
> $$dY(t)=\left[\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}(t,X(t))+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(t,X(t))\,\alpha(t)+\frac12\frac{\partial^2\varphi}{\partial x^2}(t,X(t))\,\beta^2(t)\right]dt+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(t,X(t))\,\beta(t)\,dW(t).$$

**Why it differs from the chain rule:** deterministically only first-order terms matter; stochastically $(dX)^2 = \beta^2(dW)^2 = \beta^2dt$ is of order $dt$, so a **second-order correction** survives: $\frac12\varphi_{xx}\beta^2dt$. Mnemonic (Taylor + multiplication table):
$$dY = \varphi_t\,dt+\varphi_x\,dX+\tfrac12\varphi_{xx}(dX)^2,\qquad (dt)^2=0,\ dt\,dW=0,\ (dW)^2=dt.$$
**MC trap:** the correct answer has the factor $\frac12$ in front of $\varphi_{xx}\beta^2$, $\varphi_x$ multiplying both $\alpha$ (in $dt$) and $\beta$ (in $dW$), and **no** $\varphi_{xx}$ term in the $dW$ part.

**Standard applications (know by heart):**
| $Y$ | $dY$ |
|---|---|
| $W^2$ | $dt + 2W\,dW$ (⇒ $\int_0^tW\,dW = \frac12(W(t)^2-t)$) |
| $\ln S$, $dS=\mu S\,dt+\sigma S\,dW$ | $(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2)dt+\sigma\,dW$ |
| $S^n$ | $nS^n(\mu+\frac12(n-1)\sigma^2)dt + n\sigma S^n dW$ |
| $e^{-rt}S$ | $e^{-rt}S[(\mu-r)dt+\sigma dW]$ |
| $1/S$ | $\frac1S[(-\mu+\sigma^2)dt-\sigma\,dW]$ |
| $e^{\sigma W - \frac12\sigma^2 t}$ | $\sigma Y\,dW$ (martingale) |

**Two-dimensional Itô (product rule) — useful extra:** $d(XY) = X\,dY+Y\,dX+dX\,dY$; with $X$ deterministic (e.g. $e^{-rt}$), $dX\,dY = 0$.

## 14.13 Example 152 — from log-returns to prices
If $\eta(t) = \alpha t+\beta W(t)$ (generalized BM) and $S(t)=S_0e^{\eta(t)}$, Itô with $\varphi(x)=S_0e^x$ ($\varphi_x=\varphi_{xx}=\varphi$):
$$dS(t) = S(t)\Big[\big(\alpha+\tfrac12\beta^2\big)dt+\beta\,dW(t)\Big],$$
i.e. Black–Scholes dynamics $dS = S(\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW)$ with $\mu=\alpha+\frac12\beta^2$, $\sigma=\beta$.

## 14.14 Diffusions and SDEs — Definition 153 [BOR26]
$X$ is a **diffusion** if it solves the SDE
$$dX(t)=a(t,X(t))\,dt+b(t,X(t))\,dW(t)\iff X(t)=x_0+\int_0^ta(s,X(s))\,ds+\int_0^tb(s,X(s))\,dW(s),$$
with $a,b$ **deterministic functions** of time and the **current** value of $X$. Diffusions are special Itô processes (coefficients depend only on $t$ and $X(t)$, Markov property). The Black–Scholes stock is a diffusion ($a=\mu x$, $b=\sigma x$). Other examples: arithmetic BM ($a=\mu$, $b=\sigma$), Ornstein–Uhlenbeck/Vasicek ($a=\kappa(\theta-x)$, $b=\sigma$).

## 14.15 Takeaways
Filtrations = flow of information; BM = basic risk factor; Itô integrals aggregate shocks; Itô processes = drift + diffusion; Itô's formula = key computational tool; SDEs/diffusions = framework for Black–Scholes.
